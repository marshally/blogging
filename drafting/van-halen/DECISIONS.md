# Decision Log

Settled choices, in chronological order, with rationale. Don't relitigate without new information.

## D1. Framework selection: Belbin as cast, Bales as plot
Ranked candidates for fit to a named-character role mapping:
1. **Belbin's nine team roles** — chosen as the character sheet. Only framework built as a
   typology of named roles in a working team; core thesis (balanced > all-star, uncovered/
   duplicated roles kill teams, Apollo syndrome) is literally the post's argument.
2. **Bales' task vs. social-emotional split** — chosen as the plot/thesis engine. Two poles,
   almost never the same person, group fate = their cooperation.
3. Benne & Sheats (1948) — demoted to citation-footnote ancestry; 26 roles ≠ "super simple."
4. Jehn's conflict research — one inline clause (task conflict fuels, relationship conflict kills).
5. Woolley / Project Aristotle — used only as the honest complication in the conclusion;
   actively cuts against the role-composition framing.
6. Expectation states / role emergence — cut entirely; different blog post.

## D2. Keep all nine Belbin roles uncompressed
Do not merge or rename roles into a simplified 4–5 role model. Present the real taxonomy;
simply don't use every role. Rationale: readers who know Belbin will trust the piece;
the *unused* roles (empty seats) become findings.

## D3. Central thesis: the contested Shaper seat
The Eddie/Roth conflict is framed as **two claimants competing for one Shaper seat**
(Belbin's known combustible duplication), not as a Plant-vs-Resource-Investigator
personality clash. Corollary: every post-1985 frontman is implicitly "the new guy,"
entering after the contest is settled and structurally unable to threaten Eddie's seat.
This resolves the Shaper mapping ambiguity by making it the story's engine.
Key claim derived from it: **sequence, not temperament, is the variable** (Hagar).

## D4. Sourcing discipline: no inner motives, hard references only
Never assert band members' inner motives or private psychology as fact. Structural claims
and observable record only: interviews, memoirs, liner notes, contracts, public letters.
Where accounts conflict (Hagar vs. brothers on the 1996 exit), present the conflict of
accounts as data. Use "reportedly" for secondary-sourced claims (piped-in vocals,
Anthony's contract terms).

## D5. The "two scoreboards" layer
Fan-love vs. peer-admiration as two legibility systems. Gap is largest for Implementer
(Alex) and Teamworker (Anthony) roles. Software mapping: promotion-committee problem;
users/management read scoreboard 1, engineers read scoreboard 2. Anthony is the
hardest-sourced case (lead with him); Alex's case rests partly on softer forum/listicle
evidence (frame via drummer publications and musicians calling him underrated, not
direct claims about fan opinion).

## D6. Ending B: two scoreboards / fog, not succession / elegy
The post ends on the fog callback carrying the two-scoreboards thesis (a lens for the
reader's own team), NOT on Eddie's death and succession (a feeling about Eddie).
Rationale: the author cares about the Eddie feeling personally, but readers come for
software commentary. Wolfgang / 2012–15 / 2020 compressed to a few restrained
sentences, no elegy. Avoids the piece "ending twice."

## D7. Structural devices (loaded guns)
- **"Jump" reveal:** the cold-open soundtrack choice is revealed in Act I to be the
  decisive artifact of the Shaper war (5150 side-branch ship). Do not foreshadow in §1.
- **Fog = thesis in image form:** spectacle scoreboard wiped out, practitioner scoreboard
  intact because the author stood close enough with trained eyes.
- **Shaper seat left conspicuously unassigned** in the cast sheet as the hook into Act I.
- **Anthony planted in Act I** as the inward-facing social-emotional pole, so his Act III
  firing lands as payoff, not new claim.
- **Monitor Evaluator vacancy foreshadowed** in cast sheet, paid off at Van Halen III.

# Sources

## Memoirs and books (load-bearing)
- Sammy Hagar, *Red: My Uncensored Life in Rock* (2011) — Leffler, Danniels, the 1996
  exit (his account), Hagar-era dynamics.
- David Lee Roth, *Crazy from the Heat* (1997).
- Noel Monk, *Runnin' with the Devil* (2017) — Roth-era manager's account. CAUTION:
  fired party with a perspective.
- Ted Templeman with Greg Renoff, *A Platinum Producer's Life in Music* (2020) —
  production dynamics, "Jump"/synth resistance, siding with Roth on direction.
- Greg Renoff, *Van Halen Rising* (2015) — best-researched early history.
- Alex Van Halen, *Brothers* (2024).

## Primary documents
- Roth open letter, October 1996 (widely reprinted) — VMA reunion collapse, his account.
- Eddie Van Halen, Billboard interview, 2015 — "he does not want to be my friend"
  (the post's only direct quote); criticism of Roth's live performances.
- *Van Halen III* liner notes/credits (1998) — Eddie on bass (Anthony on 3 tracks),
  Eddie lead vocal on "How Many Say I," Mike Post co-production.
- Gary Cherone, VHND retrospective interview, January 17, 2013 — called *Van Halen III*
  "really Eddie's baby," said the album needed more guidance, and contrasted its studio
  process with becoming a band on tour.
- Gary Cherone with Greg Prato, VHND interview, April 10, 2023 — described the album as
  a studio production made while he was still getting to know the band and described the
  subsequent writing process with Eddie.
- Eddie Van Halen, Guitar World, Feb 2014 (Chris Gill; VHND repost 2014-01-02) —
  Minimoog riff "more like a Peter Gabriel song" spent on "Dancing in the Street";
  "I wanted more control. I was always butting heads with Ted Templeman about what
  makes a good record." Used in the Interlude.
- "I [f—ing] hated that song. I never wanted to do it. That was the reason I decided
  to build my own studio." — UNSOURCED; circulates via Fandom wiki/VHND/Far Out with
  no dated interview. Used in the Interlude WITH an explicit folklore flag; do not
  cite as sourced.
- 5150 Studios name/timeline (police code via Donn Landee's scanner; built early 1983;
  *1984* first album recorded there) — Wikipedia "5150 Studios," Songfacts, Eddie
  Trunk Jan 2014.

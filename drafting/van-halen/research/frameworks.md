# Academic Frameworks: Considered, Ranked, Used

## Used in the post

### Belbin's Team Roles (the cast)
Meredith Belbin, *Management Teams: Why They Succeed or Fail* (1981), from studies of
management teams at Henley, 1970s–80s. Nine roles in three clusters:

**Thinking:** Plant (creative source; Eddie), Monitor Evaluator (dispassionate critic;
VACANT — a finding), Specialist (narrow deep expertise; unassigned).
**Action:** Shaper (the driver; THE CONTESTED SEAT — Eddie vs. Roth), Implementer
(plans → reliable execution; Alex), Completer Finisher (end-of-pipeline perfectionist;
Eddie's second role in the studio).
**People:** Coordinator (chairperson; outsourced to Templeman then Leffler — a finding),
Teamworker (glue; Michael Anthony), Resource Investigator (outward-facing opportunity
energy; Roth, then Hagar — recast from "Coordinator-adjacent" 2026-07-13 per D21;
see hagar-resource-investigator.md).

Key theses used: teams fail from uncovered and duplicated roles, not talent deficits;
Apollo syndrome (all-star teams underperform balanced ones); two Shapers = known
combustible configuration.

Caveat carried in the post: the Belbin Self-Perception Inventory has real psychometric
critics (Furnham et al., 1990s). Used as a lens, not a diagnostic.

### Bales' Interaction Process Analysis (the plot)
Robert F. Bales, *Interaction Process Analysis* (1950), Harvard. Groups reliably
differentiate into a task specialist and a social-emotional specialist; almost never the
same person; group fate hangs on their cooperation. One of the most replicated findings
in small-group research; fed the Ohio State leadership studies (initiating structure vs.
consideration).

Post-specific application: Roth held the *outward* social-emotional face (audience);
Anthony quietly held the *inward* face (band). Roth was never content to be only the
social pole — a Shaper claimant.

### Jehn's conflict research (one clause)
Karen Jehn: task conflict can improve output; relationship conflict destroys it.
Used at the Act I pivot (contest decided → conflict migrates task → relationship → split)
and implicitly in Act II.

### Woolley / Project Aristotle (the honest complication)
Anita Woolley et al., "Evidence for a Collective Intelligence Factor in the Performance
of Human Groups," *Science* (2010): group performance tracks social sensitivity,
turn-taking equality, composition — more than average IQ. Google's Project Aristotle:
psychological safety. Used once, in the conclusion, as the concession that the role lens
is a lens — with the turn that even this research measures what no scoreboard was measuring.

## Considered and cut

- **Benne & Sheats, "Functional Roles of Group Members" (1948).** The founding taxonomy
  (task roles / group-building roles / self-centered roles; harmonizer ≈ glue, evaluator ≈
  analyst). Cut: 26 roles, designed for discussion groups; Belbin covers the need.
- **Expectation States Theory (Berger, Cohen, Zelditch).** How people *end up* in roles
  (status characteristics → early deference → crystallized roles). Different blog post.
- **"Babble hypothesis"** (talkativeness predicts leadership emergence). Unused.
- **Humor-role literature** (group clown/joker as tension-release; Terrion & Ashforth on
  putdown humor in cohesive groups). Unused — no clown mapping in final piece.
- **Stewart's team-design meta-analyses; personality-mix composition research.** Unused.

## Ranking rationale (why Belbin + Bales)
Judged on one criterion: cleanest map onto named characters playing distinct roles.
Belbin is the only framework that IS such a typology; Bales supplies the narrative engine
(two poles, one plot). Benne & Sheats too sprawling; Jehn is evidence not structure;
Woolley actively cuts against role-composition framing; expectation states answers a
different question. Full ranking preserved in DECISIONS.md D1.

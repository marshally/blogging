- **Located, publisher page not loaded: *A Cook's Tour*, Bloomsbury USA, 2001.** Same evidence as above.

### Style (editorial reading)

Truth through appetite. A meal or a place is the way in, and the real subject turns out to be history, class, labor, or grief.

- **First-person and confessional.** He's in the scene and often looks bad. That self-deprecation earns him the right to judge other things harshly.
- **Dignity for the people doing the work.** Line cooks, street vendors, and dishwashers get respect. Status gets none unless it's earned.
- **Unsentimental but generous.** He refuses both the tourist-brochure version and easy cynicism. Endings often land on ambivalence rather than a moral.
- **Varied sentence rhythm.** Short, blunt jabs next to long sentences that pile up sensory detail.
- **The unannounced turn.** A piece that seems to be about noodles arrives at war, colonialism, or what it means to be a guest. The deeper point is walked into, not stated up front.

### Style (editorial reading)

Delight as a teaching tool. He makes the reader feel smart for being curious, then shows that what they "knew" was wrong.

- **Starts from a weird, concrete example** and climbs to the general principle.
- **Mythbusting without contempt.** The person holding the folk belief is treated as reasonable, just misinformed.
- **Unexpected pop-culture analogies.** Broadway, sitcoms, and cartoons sit next to historical linguistics. The jump in register is both the joke and the lesson.
- **A talky, digressive voice** that admits where experts still disagree.
- **Change as life, not decay.** The deeper message is optimistic.

What the two styles share: generosity toward both the reader and the subject, a concrete specific before any thesis, and seriousness carried lightly. Ullman and Ford come closest to combining them.

## Candidate moves for the primer (suggestions, not approved)

Each move is a suggestion for Marshall to accept, reject, or reshape. Per AGENTS.md rule 5, the book's framing and extended metaphors remain his choice.

1. **Open lessons on a scene, not a thesis.** For example, an agent burning 200,000 tokens rediscovering a module it already read. Let the principle (deep modules, progressive disclosure) arrive as the explanation. *(Bourdain's unannounced turn.)*
2. **Start from the folk belief and debunk it kindly.** For example, "more context is always better" or "agents make fundamentals obsolete." The reader who believed it is treated as reasonable. *(McWhorter's mythbusting without contempt.)*
3. **Give credit to the classics' authors as people.** Ousterhout, Parnas, and Brooks show up as practitioners who solved a real problem, not as citations. *(Bourdain's dignity for the people doing the work.)*
4. **One unexpected analogy per lesson,** drawn from outside software, used to land a point and then dropped. This is a small device, distinct from an extended metaphor, which stays Marshall's call.
5. **Put yourself in the scene when it went wrong.** A first-person failure with an agent earns the authority for the prescription that follows. *(Bourdain's confessional stance.)*
6. **Admit where practitioners disagree.** Böckeler's TDD experiment ([further-authors.md](further-authors.md#birgitta-böckeler)) is a natural place to show the open question rather than flatten it. *(McWhorter's honesty about unsettled questions.)*

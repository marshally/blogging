FollowAction is a current follower counter embedded in fresh post metadata. It is unrelated to the post publication date. Connections remain unknown and were not substituted for followers. Peter Isberg’s [current GitHub profile](https://github.com/PIsberg) links the exact LinkedIn account. Exact-handle searches found a matching publisher biography and no usable author posts or follower number. Namesakes were excluded.

The fourteen baseline records include Barbini’s code-quality article distribution, four Osmani posts about agent loops, tutorials and a livestream, Kim’s summit invitation, Yegge’s governance essay distribution, Lelek’s book deal and travel-assistant demonstration, and five Skowroński posts covering manuscript completion and the countdown campaign. The Polish and English Day 15 posts have separate activity IDs and remain separate records. Native document embeds are recorded as documents, including Osmani’s Practical Loop Engineering post and Skowroński’s countdown materials. The baseline does not establish a native short-video cadence.

The counts are platform snapshots rather than performance forecasts. The sample was selected from existing dossier URLs because they illustrate marketing and AI topics. It has no sampling frame, pagination exhaustion, baseline boundary proof, or representativeness claim. Additional older post responses were archived as identity and current-counter leads but excluded from baseline content records. All rate and growth targets are unmet coverage targets for coordinator treatment.

Every baseline post has an archived original response supporting its date and public reactions/comments. LikeAction is recorded as reactions because the same HTML labels its total “Reactions.” Reported comments include author comments. Views, repost totals, and comments excluding the author are unknown, rather than zero. Publicly rendered comments are a limited subset and cannot establish total non-author comments.

Collection produced seven account entities, seven account coverage records, fourteen content records, thirty-nine captures, and eighty-four observations. Thirty-four observations have accepted archive-backed numeric values; fifty are explicitly unavailable. Named-schema validation and the repository validator with root context passed. The validator’s manual full-document warnings arise from unavailable observations’ failure/source receipts, not accepted numeric evidence. Accepted numeric observations use exact excerpts and CSS locators inside original HTML.

The fourteen blocked profile/activity requests returned nonstandard HTTP 999. Frozen schema permits HTTP 100–599, so corrected receipts have new UUIDs, null http_status, and an explicit failure reason. Each preserves the complete error response and the original invalid pre-validation receipt bytes as a supplemental tool_response artifact. This is a documented pre-validation replacement, not a claim that the original HTTP status was unknown. The exact-handle web-search return is archived as tool_response and supports no accepted numeric observation.

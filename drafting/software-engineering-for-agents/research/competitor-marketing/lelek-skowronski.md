# Tomasz Lelek / Artur Skowroński: social and content marketing

Research snapshot: October 13, 2026. Priority window: July 16–October 13, 2026. This is an internal competitive memo for *Standing on the Shoulders of Giants: A Software Engineering Primer for Agentic Coding*. It examines observable practices, not the authors' private strategy or commercial results. Sources are first-party author, publisher, platform, employer, and event pages. Recommendations below are proposed tests, not changes to Marshall's approved marketing plan.

## Decisive findings

**This team supplies a useful model for connecting engineering education to consulting.** Skowroński publishes technical curation under his own name, teaches book ideas on LinkedIn, and develops related arguments on VirtusLab's site. Those company articles point to Visdom, which offers assessment, implementation, advisory work, and ongoing support. This is a visible path to an employer's commercial offer. It does not establish that the book generated consulting clients, or that those clients belong to either author personally. [S09, S13, S23]

**The current launch uses substantial native LinkedIn posts with slide documents.** On September 28, Skowroński announced an intended daily, 30-day countdown. The first post combines an argument about unverified generated work with a ten-page presentation. The October 7 installment has nine pages. These attachment counts come from LinkedIn's public native-document metadata. The inspected examples establish a series in progress, not completion of all 30 installments. [S19, S20]

**They adapt across languages and audiences.** Skowroński posted English and Polish versions of Day 15 on October 12, approximately five minutes apart. VirtusLab published related English and Polish banking-governance articles a week apart in September. The newsletter addresses JVM practitioners, while the company articles increasingly address organizational buyers. [S15, S16, S21, S22]

**Lelek and Skowroński bring different distribution assets.** Lelek explicitly connects the new title to his earlier book with Jon Skeet and continues promoting that backlist. Skowroński brings JVM Weekly, a conference portfolio, employer publishing, and the countdown campaign. Treating the pair as one identical social persona would hide the division of work visible in this sample. [S02–S06, S10, S25]

**Short-video production remains unestablished.** There is evidence for long-form interviews, public slide decks, native document posts, technical newsletters, and workshops. This audit did not establish an article-to-short-video pipeline or a sustained Shorts, TikTok, or Reels program. Marshall's proposed 2–10 complete video lessons per article is therefore a testable delivery choice, not a practice this competitor has already proved successful. [S17, S19, S20, S24, S25]

## Launch, reuse, and editorial practice

The observable sequence runs from early-access announcement in December 2025 through chapter updates, explanatory articles, speaking invitations, manuscript completion, and a pre-release countdown. The February update explicitly presents feedback as a reader benefit. It also combines educational differentiation with a discount, while July's Lelek promotion shows the earlier title remaining in circulation. These are distinct reasons to publish rather than repeated identical launch announcements. [S05, S06, S10, S12, S19]

There is a schedule mismatch worth preserving. The September 28 post says the planned release is 30 days away. The current Manning page estimates November. Use the publisher's current estimate for status and the social post for evidence of the campaign's framing. Neither establishes a final release date. [S01, S19]

The strongest reuse evidence is concrete. Day 15 appears in two languages with separate URLs and timestamps: English at 15:10:27 UTC and Polish at 15:15:53 UTC on October 12. The Polish text also includes additional explanation of interruptions in autonomous agent loops, so these are not perfectly identical copies. The banking articles preserve the same governance argument and commercial destination across languages. [S15, S16, S21, S22]

Other reuse has a narrower evidentiary basis. The July newsletter directly links the book and a VirtusLab article while discussing executable specifications. The May company essay explicitly develops ideas from the book. The June tour invitation says its conversation draws on that book. These are verified connections. It is an inference that a coordinated editorial operation deliberately moves every idea through all these channels. [S07–S09]

The newsletter's September 3 issue makes a different kind of reuse explicit: Skowroński says Trisha Gee authorized a complete republication, then adds a roundup. This is useful competitive evidence for the primer. Recognizable masters and durable engineering practices already appear in a competitor's free content. It does not prove the book itself is organized around the classics. [S14]

Native document posts are the clearest compact visual format found. Day 1 and Day 10 include ten- and nine-page presentations respectively, accompanied by substantial feed text. Their public HTML identifies them as presentations. This audit did not inspect every slide or infer completion rates. GOTO provides a historical long-form interview/transcript model for Lelek's earlier title. None of these observations establishes short-video repurposing. [S19, S20, S24]

## Practices worth testing for Marshall

These tests fit the existing articles → 2–10 short lessons → talks → book → workshops → course sequence, with consulting as the main commercial objective.

1. **Connect a lesson to a diagnosable team problem.** Skowroński's company material connects verification and context failures to a concrete engagement. For one primer article, add an ungated check a reader can run against an actual repository, then a clear contact path for help interpreting the result. Measure substantive replies and qualified consulting conversations. Enabling advantage to acknowledge: VirtusLab already has an implementation offering and delivery team. Marshall should promise only an assessment or service he can actually deliver. [S13, S23]
2. **Package several complete lessons as a recognizable sequence.** Test three lessons from one article with consistent titles and distinct demonstrations. Keep Marshall's planned short videos as the primary experiment, with one companion document adaptation if useful. Measure relevant replies and repeat viewers where available. Their countdown shows packaging, not that daily publishing or slide documents outperform video. [S19–S22]
3. **Show the same principle in practitioner and buyer language.** An engineer sees the failing test or architecture boundary. A manager sees review queues, onboarding cost, or incident uncertainty. Test two substantive adaptations of one lesson and record which produces useful conversations. Their enabling advantage is access to employer-specific industry material. Marshall needs his own supportable examples, not their banking claims. [S09, S15, S16]
4. **Make a workshop promise narrow enough to inspect.** Confitura's listing names the intended senior audience, prerequisites, duration, and practical system participants would build. Test interest in one bounded primer exercise before creating a course. A listing with ten seats is evidence of offer design, not ten buyers. [S17]
5. **Make attribution part of the lesson's value.** The Trisha Gee issue shows that a familiar engineering idea can remain relevant content. For Marshall, retain the stronger organizing promise: named source, agent instruction, demonstration, and observable check. Test whether readers actually apply the principle, rather than counting recognition of a famous name. Their newsletter relationship and permission to republish another author's essay are enabling assets, not automatically reproducible advantages. [S14]

## Evidence limits and do-not-use ledger

- **Verified primary evidence:** page ownership/author attribution, public post text, exact LinkedIn publication metadata, presentation attachment metadata, advertised offers, and the publisher's current early-access state. Statements about AI productivity or customer outcomes inside marketing copy were not independently validated here.
- **Inference:** division of promotional roles, audience progression, and the applicability of these practices to Marshall. No causal claim about reach, sales, or consulting conversion is supported.
- **Coverage limits:** public pages and search are incomplete. X yielded no useful timeline. No comprehensive personal short-video program or current book webinar was verified. Do not rewrite that as “they do not use video.”
- **Identity exclusion:** `artur-skowronski.pl` belongs to an unrelated graphic designer. Do not attach that site or art portfolio to this engineer. Lelek also has namesakes in games/art; use the GitHub and publisher cross-links above.
- **Attribution exclusion:** VirtusLab/Visdom subscribers and leads are company assets. GOTO viewers are the host's audience. Dremio affiliation does not demonstrate employer promotion of this book.
- **Date exclusion:** crawler-relative LinkedIn ages and search “published” labels were unreliable. The book's November publication is estimated. Workshop listings and invitations do not prove attendance or completed delivery.
- **Metric exclusion:** no growth rates, engagement-rate comparisons, sales estimates, paid-ad claims, algorithm claims, or revenue attribution. Historical discounts describe the inspected promotion, not a currently redeemable offer.

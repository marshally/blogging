# Gene Kim / Steve Yegge: content and social distribution

Research date: October 13, 2026. Internal research memo for *Standing on the Shoulders of Giants: A Software Engineering Primer for Agentic Coding*. Recent observation window: July 16–October 13, 2026, supplemented with the 2025 book campaign. Public evidence only. “Observed path” means linked destinations and offers were inspected, not that a reader converted.

## Decisive findings

**This competitor combines two author practices with a publisher and events business.** Yegge's current site leads explicitly from technical writing to organizational AI consulting. Kim connects practitioner education to IT Revolution's books, events, community, and executive development. Their joint book and video series connect those audiences. Treating all this as two people posting to LinkedIn would badly understate the resources involved. [Yegge services](https://yegge.ai/services), [Kim's event invitation](https://itrevolution.com/articles/enterprise-ai-summit-comes-to-bletchley-park-this-november/), [joint book and videos](https://yegge.ai/vibe-coding).

**Reuse is established, but a systematic short-video strategy is not.** The publisher explicitly describes turning books and papers into short, pain-point-focused blog articles. The authors recorded a cohosted video series around sections of their book. These are direct statements about reuse. The inspected evidence does not establish an article-to-2–10-short-clips production workflow, a regular Shorts/Reels/TikTok cadence, or the commercial effectiveness of such a workflow. [Publisher's September 2 disclosure](https://itrevolution.com/articles/how-we-use-ai-at-it-revolution-a-transparent-look-at-ai-and-writing/), [Kim's first-episode announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/realgenekim_s1e1-when-and-how-vibe-coding-changed-our-activity-7351109780913934338-r0E6).

**Yegge is a close business-model comparison for Marshall.** His owned archive presents technical evidence and sends visitors toward keynotes, leadership conversations, and workshops. A separate CIO landing page organizes the offer around organizational situations: skepticism, moving too fast, and pilots that stall. This is a visible consulting lead path. Revenue attributable to it is unknown. [CIO page](https://yegge.ai/cio).

## Launch and reuse: what can actually be traced?

**The 2025 campaign serialized the book's ideas before and after publication.** The publisher archive lists recognizable excerpts spanning June through December: definition, mindset, value, skills, workflow, culture, standards, hiring, and curricula. The sequence gave multiple subjects their own discoverable article. This is observed packaging, not evidence that an editorial calendar or specific interval caused sales. [Joint-author archive](https://itrevolution.com/author/steve-yegge/).

Kim's [first video announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/realgenekim_s1e1-when-and-how-vibe-coding-changed-our-activity-7351109780913934338-r0E6) explicitly describes conversations about book sections, including material that did not make the book. He states a goal of recording every couple of days and says six episodes were already recorded. That establishes launch intent and batching, not sustained cadence. The [book's video index](https://yegge.ai/vibe-coding) shows FAAFO, the head-chef metaphor, failure stories, writing the book, and live demos. **The strongest supported pattern is one body of research expressed as excerpts, conversation, demonstration, and workshop material.** No evidence here supports assigning a fixed number of clips to each article.

The publisher's later disclosure removes another uncertainty. Brown describes a small editorial team using AI to adapt its authors' existing books and papers into blog-length material, followed by authorial responsibility and human editorial checks. That is a documented production capability. It should not be credited as a personal daily-writing routine for Kim or Yegge. [September 2 policy and workflow account](https://itrevolution.com/articles/how-we-use-ai-at-it-revolution-a-transparent-look-at-ai-and-writing/).

Yegge's recent factory material travels differently. “Fences” appears on his site, gets a thesis-and-link LinkedIn post, and is linked from Gas City's blog. September's podcast covers related factory failures and governance themes. The links prove distribution and thematic reuse. They do not prove a coordinated campaign or that the podcast was derived from a particular essay. [LinkedIn announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/steveyegge_fences-not-sandboxes-steve-yegge-activity-7497700682158833664-eqYK), [company blog](https://blog.gascity.com/), [host episode](https://re-cinq.com/podcast/steve-yegge-software-factory).

## Observed conversion paths

1. **Essay → consulting engagement.** Yegge's essays finish with a services link. Services distinguishes keynotes, bespoke meetings, and joint workshops, then asks the buyer to describe their organization's situation. The CIO page offers problem-based navigation and a conversation CTA. The value proposition is organizational change informed by hands-on engineering. No booking was submitted. [Services](https://yegge.ai/services), [CIO page](https://yegge.ai/cio).
2. **Social invitation → event application.** Kim's Bletchley LinkedIn post points to a dated publisher article, which explains the audience and directs readers to apply. The article explicitly describes limited attendance and rolling application review. This is a qualified event funnel, not an unrestricted newsletter signup. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/realgenekim_enterprise-ai-summit-comes-to-bletchley-park-activity-7490901940969725952-5n6y), [article](https://itrevolution.com/articles/enterprise-ai-summit-comes-to-bletchley-park-this-november/).
3. **Book interest → resources, workshop, community.** The publisher lists an excerpt, reader's guide, measurement toolkit, failure-pattern guide, and developer-loops resource. The authors' book page connects the book to joint workshops. Kim's launch-community article invites Slack participation and an event workshop. The book therefore has several participation paths beyond purchase. Email gating of individual downloads was not verified. [Book product](https://itrevolution.com/product/vibe-coding-book/), [community invitation](https://itrevolution.com/articles/join-vibe-coding-community/).
4. **Existing event relationship → paid peer learning.** The Monthly AI Forum specifically targets prior ETLS leaders and people they bring. It advertises experience reports, confidential questions, breakouts, and member summaries. Listed annual prices are $5,000 for an individual seat, $3,000 per seat for teams of 2–4, and $2,000 for teams of 5+. These are observed offers, not realized revenue. [Membership page](https://events.itrevolution.com/monthly-ai-forum/).
5. **Technical artifact → adjacent product relationship.** Beads has a public installation path and docs. Gas City's blog offers email subscription and links to commercial product pages. Beads Team Server asks for work email to request early access, with managed and self-hosted positioning. Yegge is an advisor, so do not attribute this company's sales or mailing list to him. [Beads repository](https://github.com/gastownhall/beads), [early-access offer](https://gascity.com/beads-team-server/), [company roles](https://gascity.com/about/).

The public [Beads repository](https://github.com/gastownhall/beads) displayed approximately **27.8k stars and 1.9k forks** when retrieved October 13, 2026. These are rounded platform counters for a software project, not newsletter subscribers, active users, buyers, or attributable book demand. Relevant advantages precede the book: Yegge's owned archive reaches back to 2004, while Kim [launched The Idealcast in May 2020](https://itrevolution.com/articles/announcing-the-idealcast/) with established expert guests and associated conference talks. IT Revolution can offer editorial work, existing event relationships, and paid peer groups. The transferable mechanism is connecting useful expertise with a fitting next step. The prerequisite network, reputation, staff capacity, and event infrastructure are not instantly reproducible.

## Transferable tests for Marshall

These are proposals, not changes to the approved marketing plan or platform policy. Preserve the consulting-led sequence: articles → 2–10 complete short lessons → talks → book → workshops → course.

1. **Test one consulting path at the end of one strong lesson.** Pair a classic engineering principle with a concrete agent failure and a reproducible correction. Link to an existing owned page explaining the kinds of team problems Marshall can help assess. Measure relevant conversations and requests for help, not only clicks. Yegge's problem-oriented buyer navigation is the useful model. A large archive is not required to test this.
2. **Test a complete reuse chain before increasing production.** Select one article and make two short videos, one showing the failure and one demonstrating the check. Link the evidence and source classic from the article. Record production time, substantive replies, and whether viewers can apply the check. Kim/Yegge support reuse across formats, but they do not supply evidence that Marshall's proposed short-video ratio will work.
3. **Publish a small usable artifact alongside the explanation.** A review checklist, before/after diff, or agent instruction with an observable acceptance check can make the intellectual promise inspectable. OSS in the Yegge ecosystem provides a stronger form of this proof, but maintaining a platform is unnecessary for the primer's first test. Measure reports of actual application.
4. **Let workshop questions generate follow-up material.** When the planned live-workshop stage arrives, collect repeated difficulties and refine the relevant lessons. The publisher's community and membership paths illustrate several ways of turning participation into further learning. Marshall can start with one bounded workshop rather than creating an ongoing membership obligation.
5. **Use guest appearances as distribution experiments.** Pitch one precise demonstration to a suitable existing technical show or conference after publishing the supporting article. Yegge's guest archive shows the role of external hosts. His access to established hosts is an advantage, not a forecast for Marshall. Track invitations and relevant follow-up separately from video views.

## Evidence limits and do-not-use ledger

- **Verified primary:** directly fetched author, project, company, event, and platform pages. Publisher pages returning 403 were read through search-indexed text of those primary URLs. Those establish published claims and offers, with weaker freshness assurance than a successful live fetch.
- **Interpretation:** audience roles, positioning, competitive implications, and the proposed tests. They are analytical judgments. The competitors' AI-performance assertions are evidence of their messaging, not independently validated engineering results.
- **Social dates:** LinkedIn's public relative timestamps varied between snapshots. None was converted into an exact publication date. A related article's explicit date is not assigned automatically to a LinkedIn post. The dated Bluesky example has an explicit timestamp.
- **Ownership:** IT Revolution staff content, hosted guest interviews, and Gas City product marketing are separately attributed. Gas City's current team page lists Yegge as advisor. His latest essays describe Wheelhouse as his own closed-source working environment, so calling Gas Town his only current system would be stale.
- **Newsletter:** Yegge considering a weekly newsletter does not prove that one exists. A visible subscription form does not establish list size, email cadence, or the messages subscribers receive.
- **Metrics:** exclude stale LinkedIn search-card counts, Social Blade estimates, aggregated cross-platform audience sums, and revenue inferred from followers or stars. Membership price is not membership revenue.
- **Launch dates:** early video-promotion copy anticipated September 2025. The publisher product page gives October 21, 2025. Preserve that distinction rather than forcing all references into a single launch date.
- **No short-video conclusion:** verified cohosted episodes and guest distribution do not establish a current vertical-video production system. The observed evidence supports experimentation, not a claim that the closest competitor has validated Marshall's planned clip strategy.

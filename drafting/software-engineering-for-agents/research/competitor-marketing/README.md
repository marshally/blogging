Research snapshot: **October 13, 2026**. Five separate investigations of the competitors Marshall selected in [D31](../../DECISIONS.md#d31-competitor-marketing-deep-dives). The priority window is July 16–October 13, supplemented by earlier launches and examples that establish content reuse. This extends the [book positioning analysis](../competitive-analysis.md) into public social activity, publishing, and commercial offers.

**Barbini and Yegge provide the clearest models for the primer's consulting objective.** Barbini connects practical teaching with a specific team-training offer. Yegge gives readers an explicit route from technical writing to organizational help. Osmani supplies strong examples of reusable teaching, Lelek/Skowroński of serialized launch content and company distribution, and Isberg of inspectable tools connected to the book. These are comparisons of visible practices, not rankings of marketing effectiveness.

## What this means for positioning

**Engineering judgment is already a central message in this competitive set.** Barbini's recent essays discuss code quality and earlier methodology debates. Skowroński's newsletter republishes durable engineering advice with permission and names earlier authorities. Osmani teaches review, verification, and work in existing systems. The [book analysis](../competitive-analysis.md) also documents explicit classical lineage in Barbini, Isberg, and Kim/Yegge. A claim that competing authors overlook fundamentals would be unsupported.

The primer can make a more specific promise: teach a coherent sequence of named principles, show how each applies to an agent's failure, and give the reader an observable check. The distinction needs to be visible in a sample lesson. Attribution alone will not establish that the teaching is more useful, and this research does not establish an exclusive market position.

The same discipline applies to the commercial message. A developer may want the instruction and demonstration. An engineering leader may want help diagnosing why the team cannot review, verify, or maintain generated changes. Yegge's [CIO page](https://yegge.ai/cio) and the Visdom offer illustrate ways to make that organizational next step explicit. Their presence establishes an offer, not its conversion rate.

## A small test that fits the approved plan

These are research recommendations. They do not change the [marketing plan](../../marketing-plan.md), authorize publication, or select a new posting cadence.

1. **Choose one real team problem and one classic.** Publish a complete lesson with the failure, source principle, agent instruction, demonstration, and check. Barbini is a useful reference for the practical situation; Isberg and Osmani for inspectable supporting artifacts.
2. **Make two complete short lessons from it.** Each clip should leave the viewer able to do something. Add one substantive native LinkedIn treatment and keep a record of which article section, example, and source support each version. Osmani supplies the clearest inspected clip precedent; Skowroński supplies a series-packaging example.
3. **Give the material a durable home and a relevant next step.** Use the existing Yount Labs archive and [primer landing page](../../landing-page.md). Add a substantial sample and a clear invitation to discuss the specific team problem where appropriate. Keep book-interest signups separate from requests for organizational help.
4. **Measure useful response and production cost.** Record preparation time, substantive replies, reported applications of the check, book-interest signups, speaking invitations, and qualified consulting conversations separately. If interest centers on applying the lesson with a team, use that evidence to shape the already planned live workshop.

A five-channel publishing operation, a software product, and an ongoing paid community each add work beyond this first test. The competitor evidence supplies possible later directions; it does not establish that the primer needs all of them.

## Evidence boundaries

The dossiers distinguish directly observed primary material, author-reported claims, and our editorial interpretation. Samples were chosen to expose practices and connections, not to estimate complete posting cadence. Live platform counts are retrieval-date snapshots. Search access was incomplete for some feeds and publisher pages, and each dossier records those limits.

Book sales by channel, consulting conversion, email engagement, acquisition cost, and marketing return remain unestablished across this comparison. Visible followers, reactions, stars, offers, and advertised prices cannot fill those gaps. The value of this research is the concrete set of practices available to test.

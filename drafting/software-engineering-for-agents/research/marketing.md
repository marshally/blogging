# Marketing research and planning inputs

Checked 2026-10-12. Supports the [marketing plan](../marketing-plan.md).

## M01. LinkedIn’s investment in video

**Verified primary evidence.** In a [LinkedIn post sharing Gyanda Sachdeva’s product announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/matthew-derella-a6483316_video-consumption-on-linkedin-growing-at-activity-7306236282077073408--oCX), Matthew Derella highlights video growth. Sachdeva describes tests of a video tab, video recommendations in the feed, and an expanded full-screen vertical player. This is a historical product announcement, not a check of which features every member has today.

LinkedIn’s [March 18, 2026 announcement](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/how-linkedin-is-helping-b2b-marketers-reach-audiences-at-scale-w) describes expanded creator sponsorships and video advertising products. It supports the claim of continued investment in video. Paid advertising and sponsorship products do not establish an organic reach advantage for an independent educator.

LinkedIn’s [March 12, 2026 feed update](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed) emphasizes professional relevance, actionable ideas, and less repetitive, low-substance content. This supports giving each clip a complete useful lesson. It does not establish that short videos receive preferential distribution.

## M02. A friend’s results after 45 videos

**User-reported anecdote, independently unverified.** Marshall reported on 2026-10-12 that a friend published 45 short videos and netted the following follower counts: LinkedIn 1,700, TikTok 1,600, Instagram 700. The account identities, starting counts, time period, topics, and use of paid promotion were not supplied. Preserve the reported counts without treating them as a forecast, a measured causal effect, or a unique audience total across platforms.

## M03. Strategy and production model

**User-selected direction and hypothesis.** In the same request, Marshall proposed hundreds of short lessons, one per topic, derived at 2–10 videos per article. His sequence is articles, short videos, conference speaking, a self-published book, a publisher pitch supported by the audience, a course assembled from the videos, and an optional podcast interviewing featured authors. The professional audience’s receptiveness and the resulting opportunities remain hypotheses to test.

The plan’s subscriber path follows the existing [posting strategy](../../../docs/2026-posting-strategy.md#identity-and-conversion-paths). Measuring response, using feedback in revision, and adding course exercises are editorial recommendations implementing the direction. No posting cadence or numerical growth target has been selected.

## M04. John Sonmez: Free and paid content

**Verified primary evidence.** In [The McMethod Email Marketing Podcast, episode 106](https://www.dropdeadcopy.com/episode-106-john-sonmez-on-being-a-rockstar-in-your-niche-and-beyond/) (2015), the published interview transcript records Sonmez’s personal practice of giving away 90% of his work and charging for 10%, in a discussion of building credibility through content channels. His [own article on personal branding](https://simpleprogrammer.com/personal-brand-reputation/), under “Create Value For Others,” repeats the split. Supports the appendix’s quotation and attribution. This is a practitioner’s strategy, not a measured optimum for consulting businesses.

## M05. Ramit Sethi: Quality of free material

**Verified primary evidence.** [Being Boss, episode 57](https://beingboss.club/podcast/podcast-episode-57-i-will-teach-you-to-be-rich-ramit-sethi), dated February 2, 2016, provides a transcript with Sethi’s 90% statement at **59:24**. He follows it with his goal for free material to exceed the quality of competitors’ paid material and invites readers to evaluate his guides and emails. At **1:00:48**, he separately describes GrowthLab’s material as 98% free. Preserve the 90% quotation in its context rather than treating it as a fixed ratio across all his offerings.

## M06. Joe Pulizzi: Ungated access

**Verified primary evidence.** Adam Franklin’s [interview with Joe Pulizzi](https://www.bluewiremedia.com.au/how-to-do-content-marketing-like-the-godfather-joe-pulizzi/), posted May 7, 2013, includes the section “Give 90% away free.” Pulizzi recommends making well over 90% of content accessible without registration, arguing that forms limit sharing and discovery. Supports the appendix’s excerpt. The percentage concerns gating, not a division of knowledge into free and paid services.

**Attribution limit across M04–M06.** These are verified occurrences of related advice. None has been established as the original source of Marshall’s remembered recommendation to give away 80–90% of one’s knowledge when marketing a consulting business.

## M07. Matt Pocock on The Pragmatic Engineer

**Verified primary interview; self-reported results.** Gergely Orosz’s [“AI Skills with Matt Pocock”](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/ai-skills-with-matt-pocock), September 17, 2026. Official audio transcript: teaching and Vercel (**12:43–15:06**); partnership and sales (**18:07–20:16**); AI and declining revenue (**22:59–24:09**); strategic education (**25:30–26:10**). Supports the appendix’s account. The [YouTube edition](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4DhcSPkEbwI) uses different timing. No net profit or quantified decline is established.

**User-supplied transcript; wording requires care.** Marshall supplied a [local transcript](mattpocock_interview_pragmatic_engineer.md) on 2026-10-12 and warned that it contains many errors. Preserve the supplied file as a raw source. It has garbled names, incomplete sentences, and no speaker labels or timestamps. Use it to locate and paraphrase clear passages; verify exact quotations, uncertain names and ambiguous numbers against the recording before publication.

The passage beginning “Which is nuts” describes the financial result as life-changing and leads into his account of making material that can keep selling while he returns to his family. Near the end, the discussion beginning “Now, you're an educator at heart” explains curation as ordering dependent ideas into a learning path. These support the appendix’s additions. The stated value of human curation is Pocock’s position, not a measured comparison with AI teaching.

**Do not use without clarification.** The “30, 40 X” comparison omits a clear compensation period and basis for comparison; it does not establish a salary multiple. His tentative January/February 2023 full-course-release recollection is not a verified launch date. The December 2022 date in M08 refers specifically to the pre-release.

## M08. Total TypeScript’s pre-release launch

**Verified primary account; self-reported sales.** Joel Hooks, Pocock’s publishing partner, published [“$415k Pre-Release Launch of a Developer Education Product”](https://badass.dev/launch-of-a-developer-education-product) on December 17, 2022. He reports $415,000 gross sales and more than 1,200 learners for the first installment’s pre-release. An earlier live workshop sold all 30 seats at $1,200 list / $900 early-bird pricing. The discount mix is unspecified, so seat count times list price would not establish workshop revenue. This is early demand validation within an existing audience, not evidence that a new audience will convert similarly.

## M09. Building the course with a publishing partner

**Verified primary interview.** In [“How we Shipped Total Typescript with Matt Pocock”](https://badass.dev/creating-total-typescript-with-matt-pocock), August 14, 2023, Hooks and Pocock discuss testing topics through short videos, validating paid demand through workshops, serving experienced developers, and employer education budgets. The conversation describes complementary teaching, instructional design and commercial work. It supports the appendix’s account of the offer and partnership, not a claim that recordings alone produced the sales.

## M10. Pocock’s 2024 course-business retrospective

**Verified primary interview; self-reported results.** Cory House’s [“How to Create a Course with $2.5 Million in Sales with Matt Pocock”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XDhqcOlCDf8), October 7, 2024, establishes when that milestone was reported. Video transcript: workshop testing, partners and support (**20:59–22:20**); mailing-list campaigns (**22:50–23:31**); problem/solution exercises (**37:00–40:53**). Neither this interview nor M07 independently audits the figures.

**Editorial application of M07–M10.** A paid workshop testing demand for sequenced engineering exercises is a proposed experiment for the Primer, not a selected launch commitment. The connection between Pocock’s strategic-education emphasis and this curriculum is an inference. This case establishes that substantial sales are possible in a technical niche; it does not estimate the total course market, typical creator earnings or demand for this particular course.

## Claims to avoid

Do not promise a LinkedIn algorithm boost, extrapolate the friend’s results into a follower forecast, add platform counts as unique people, or imply that an audience guarantees conference invitations or a publishing agreement.

Do not identify any of M04–M06 as the originator of the remembered advice, treat the percentages as experimentally established, or recast Pulizzi’s ungated-content recommendation as a free/paid knowledge split.

Do not present Total TypeScript’s cumulative sales as annual revenue or personal profit, add its pre-release sales to a later cumulative total, or treat a successful creator’s results as a forecast for the Primer. Do not imply current TypeScript-course revenue is still growing when Pocock reports a decline.

**Unsubstantiated portions of the user-added course summary (2026-10-12):** M07–M10 support employer-funded training and cumulative multimillion-dollar sales. They do not establish the summary’s annual net-income claim. M08’s roughly $1,000 seat price applies to a live workshop; it does not establish a comparable price for the recorded course. The appendix links point to the relevant evidence and its limits while preserving Marshall’s wording.

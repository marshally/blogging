# Writing Style: Charity Majors

Charity Majors is co-founder and CTO of Honeycomb.io, co-author of the O'Reilly books *Database Reliability Engineering* and *Observability Engineering*, and a career infrastructure engineer who has repeatedly cycled between hands-on engineering and management (Linden Lab, Parse, Facebook infra, then Honeycomb) — the "pendulum" she is famous for writing about is autobiography before it is advice. She writes at charity.wtf (originally a self-hosted WordPress blog, later migrated to Substack), and the voice is instantly identifiable in an industry genre — engineering-leadership blogging — that trends toward hedged, consultant-safe prose: she swears at the exact moment she means something most, mints quotable one-line maxims, decorates her own arguments with sparkle emoji, and builds essays directly out of her own Twitter threads rather than smoothing them into reported prose. The corpus below skews toward her two most load-bearing subject areas — the engineer/manager pendulum and deploy culture — because that is where her citation weight concentrates; her observability and database writing (the other half of her output) is not represented here and would add technical-explainer registers this document doesn't capture.

## Corpus analyzed

1. [The Engineer/Manager Pendulum](https://charity.wtf/2017/05/11/the-engineer-manager-pendulum/) (2017) — the originating post of her most influential framework; she later calls it "probably my all time favorite," and it spawned a multi-post series plus conference talks that made "the pendulum" industry shorthand.
2. [Engineering Management: The Pendulum Or The Ladder](https://charity.wtf/2019/01/04/engineering-management-the-pendulum-or-the-ladder/) (2019) — the direct sequel that operationalized the pendulum into a career-strategy framework; widely republished and cited in EM circles (LeadDev, among others).
3. [Twin Anxieties of the Engineer/Manager Pendulum](https://charity.wtf/2022/03/24/twin-anxieties-of-the-engineer-manager-pendulum/) (2022) — third installment answering reader questions about the framework; drew a substantial Hacker News discussion five years after the original, evidence of the series' staying power.
4. [If Management Isn't A Promotion, Then Engineering Isn't A Demotion](https://charity.wtf/2020/09/06/if-management-isnt-a-promotion-then-engineering-isnt-a-demotion/) (2020) — the canonical statement of her core thesis, cross-linked from nearly every later post on management as the reference version.
5. [Every Achievement Has A Denominator](https://charity.wtf/2022/10/03/every-achievement-has-a-denominator/) (2022) — coined a phrase that circulated on its own as a standalone aphorism in engineering-leadership discourse, detached from the original post.
6. [Friday Deploy Freezes Are Exactly Like Murdering Puppies](https://charity.wtf/2019/05/01/friday-deploy-freezes-are-exactly-like-murdering-puppies/) (2019) — her most viral single post (Hacker News, Lobsters), a title that became industry shorthand for the anti-Friday-freeze position; she was still fielding rebuttals and writing follow-ups to it in 2025.
7. [On Call Shouldn't Suck: A Guide For Managers](https://charity.wtf/2020/10/03/on-call-shouldnt-suck-a-guide-for-managers/) (2020) — the canonical statement of her "handshake" model of on-call responsibility, frequently referenced in SRE and observability circles.
8. [Software Deploys and Cognitive Biases](https://charity.wtf/2021/08/27/software-deploys-and-cognitive-biases/) (2021) — structurally unique (a cognitive-bias glossary built into the argument), routinely linked as the more rigorous companion piece to the Puppies post.
9. [Why I hate the phrase "breaking down silos"](https://charity.wtf/2021/08/27/why-i-hate-the-phrase-breaking-down-silos/) (2021) — a compact, widely quoted rant representative of her jargon-skewering register, often excerpted standalone.
10. [Questionable Advice: "After Being A Manager, Can I Be Happy As A Cog?"](https://charity.wtf/2019/11/23/questionable-advice-after-being-a-manager-can-i-be-happy-as-a-cog/) (2019) — the flagship entry in her long-running advice column, the format she returned to for years, and one of its most-shared installments.

## Who is writing

Charity Majors writes as a battle-scarred infrastructure engineer and repeat founder who has been both the manager and the managed, both the person paged at 3am and the person who designed the on-call rotation — and who trusts that lived, specific experience over any framework she didn't personally stress-test. She does not write as a consultant, an academic, or a neutral surveyor of "best practices"; she writes as an advocate with a strong, stated point of view, dispensing career and organizational advice the way a blunt older colleague does at the bar after two drinks (literally the origin story of the Pendulum-or-Ladder post: "Last night I was out with a dear friend who has been an engineering manager for a year now, and by two drinks in I was rattling off a long list things I always say to newer engineering managers," [Engineering Management: The Pendulum Or The Ladder](https://charity.wtf/2019/01/04/engineering-management-the-pendulum-or-the-ladder/)). Her relationship to the reader is protective and confrontational at once: she will tell an anonymous letter-writer "YOUR COMPANY SUCKS... Fuck em. Please leave" ([Questionable Advice: "After Being A Manager, Can I Be Happy As A Cog?"](https://charity.wtf/2019/11/23/questionable-advice-after-being-a-manager-can-i-be-happy-as-a-cog/)) in the same piece that ends "Hope this helps. charity." She positions herself squarely against orthodoxy she considers harmful to engineers — the idea that management is a promotion, that hierarchy is natural, that Friday deploy freezes are caution rather than cowardice — and treats the reader as someone she is trying to talk out of a bad decision before it costs them their career or their weekend.

## Core voice attributes

1. **Profanity as thesis-level emphasis, never decoration.** Profanity in her prose lands exactly at the sentence carrying the most conviction, not scattered through connective tissue. "There's a lot but let's start with: **Fuck the whole idea that only managers get career progression.** And fuckkkk the idea you have to choose a 'lane' and grow old there." ([The Engineer/Manager Pendulum](https://charity.wtf/2017/05/11/the-engineer-manager-pendulum/)) Likewise: "It sets my fucking teeth on edge." ([Why I hate the phrase "breaking down silos"](https://charity.wtf/2021/08/27/why-i-hate-the-phrase-breaking-down-silos/)) and "Fix. That. Take some cycles off product and fix your fucking deploy pipeline." ([Friday Deploy Freezes Are Exactly Like Murdering Puppies](https://charity.wtf/2019/05/01/friday-deploy-freezes-are-exactly-like-murdering-puppies/))
2. **Tough-love direct address.** She talks straight to "you," and when a specific correspondent is in front of her she confronts them by name (or pseudonym) rather than softening into generalities: "Dear Cog?, Holy shitballs! What an amazing story! ... First of all, YOUR COMPANY SUCKS. You don't once mention your leadership — where are they in all this?" ([Questionable Advice: "After Being A Manager, Can I Be Happy As A Cog?"](https://charity.wtf/2019/11/23/questionable-advice-after-being-a-manager-can-i-be-happy-as-a-cog/))
3. **Aphorism-minting.** She writes for the screenshot: single sentences engineered to stand alone as bolded, quotable maxims, often promoted to their own header. "**Management is NOT a promotion.**" ([The Engineer/Manager Pendulum](https://charity.wtf/2017/05/11/the-engineer-manager-pendulum/)) "One of the classic failure modes of management is the empire-builder... **✨Every achievement has a denominator✨**" ([Every Achievement Has A Denominator](https://charity.wtf/2022/10/03/every-achievement-has-a-denominator/)) "Night time pages are heart attacks, not diabetes." ([On Call Shouldn't Suck: A Guide For Managers](https://charity.wtf/2020/10/03/on-call-shouldnt-suck-a-guide-for-managers/))
4. **Sparkle-and-kaomoji punctuation register.** A typographic tic almost no engineering-leadership peer uses: sparkle emoji bracketing a coined phrase, kaomoji for a shrug, emoji as a punctuation mark rather than illustration. "You will be advised to stop writing code or engineering. ✨ Fuck That. ✨" ([Engineering Management: The Pendulum Or The Ladder](https://charity.wtf/2019/01/04/engineering-management-the-pendulum-or-the-ladder/)) "Above all: ✨RAISE YOUR STANDARDS✨ for what you expect from yourselves." ([On Call Shouldn't Suck: A Guide For Managers](https://charity.wtf/2020/10/03/on-call-shouldnt-suck-a-guide-for-managers/)) "If you're looking for VP roles, it may take months to find a place you want to work at, and then they might not choose you. ¯\_(ツ)_/¯" ([Twin Anxieties of the Engineer/Manager Pendulum](https://charity.wtf/2022/03/24/twin-anxieties-of-the-engineer-manager-pendulum/))
5. **Twitter-native argument construction.** Rather than paraphrasing her own past public statements, she re-embeds the actual tweet as load-bearing evidence inside the essay — the tweet IS the thesis, not an illustration of it. The entire opening act of the Puppies post is built from a quote-tweet exchange ("VOICEOVER: 'Previously, on twitter...'"), and elsewhere she drops her own tweet in as the encapsulating line: "treat every alarm like a heart attack. _fix_ the motherfucker. i do not care if this causes product development to screech to a halt." ([On Call Shouldn't Suck: A Guide For Managers](https://charity.wtf/2020/10/03/on-call-shouldnt-suck-a-guide-for-managers/), quoting her own @mipsytipsy tweet)
6. **Candor about sourcing and generosity toward collaborators.** She flags when she can't attribute a phrase rather than silently claiming it, and credits people by name even when it costs her the cleaner line: "I did not invent this phrase, but I am unfortunately unable to credit the person I heard it from (a senior Googler). I simply think it's brilliant, and so helpful." ([Every Achievement Has A Denominator](https://charity.wtf/2022/10/03/every-achievement-has-a-denominator/)) In the Pendulum post she interrupts her own argument to h/t a peer tweeting the same idea in parallel: "(h/t to @sarahmei who was tweetstorming this up at the EXACT SAME TIME as i was writing this. Yes Virginia, internet feminists ARE linked by a mystical hive brain.)"
7. **Enumerated diagnostic lists as the argument's analytical skeleton.** The emotional case is made in prose; the operational case is made in a bulleted or numbered list immediately after — reasons, biases, roadmap steps. "Ways to begin recovering from a toxic deploy culture: Have a deploy philosophy, make sure everybody knows what it is. Be consistent. Build and deploy on every set of committed changes..." ([Friday Deploy Freezes Are Exactly Like Murdering Puppies](https://charity.wtf/2019/05/01/friday-deploy-freezes-are-exactly-like-murdering-puppies/)) Elsewhere the entire post is a list of quoted bad-faith rationalizations, each parenthetically footnoted to a named cognitive bias ([Software Deploys and Cognitive Biases](https://charity.wtf/2021/08/27/software-deploys-and-cognitive-biases/)).
8. **A combative body that ends in a warm benediction.** Pieces that spend paragraphs cursing at bad practice close with an abrupt tonal turn into tenderness — first name only, lowercase, sometimes a heart. "It isn't a promotion, so you don't have any status to give up. Do it as long as it makes you happy... Then do it all over again. <3" ([The Engineer/Manager Pendulum](https://charity.wtf/2017/05/11/the-engineer-manager-pendulum/)) The Cog letter — after "Fuck em. Please leave" — ends: "P.S. — Oh and stop fretting about 'competing' with the 20-somethings kuberneteheads, you dork. You have been learning shit your whole career and you'll learn this shit too. The tech is the easy part. The tech will always be the easy part. 🙂" ([Questionable Advice: "After Being A Manager, Can I Be Happy As A Cog?"](https://charity.wtf/2019/11/23/questionable-advice-after-being-a-manager-can-i-be-happy-as-a-cog/))

## Structure and formatting

Pieces open one of four ways, all avoiding a cold thesis statement: a cold-opened embedded tweet with a mock-TV framing device ("**VOICEOVER**: 'Previously, on twitter...'", [Friday Deploy Freezes Are Exactly Like Murdering Puppies](https://charity.wtf/2019/05/01/friday-deploy-freezes-are-exactly-like-murdering-puppies/)); a personal, slightly gossipy anecdote ("Lately I've been doing some career counseling for people off Twitter (long story)," [The Engineer/Manager Pendulum](https://charity.wtf/2017/05/11/the-engineer-manager-pendulum/)); an explicit callback that catalogues her own prior posts on the theme before adding the next installment ("A quick recap of the relevant posts: [three linked prior posts]," [Twin Anxieties of the Engineer/Manager Pendulum](https://charity.wtf/2022/03/24/twin-anxieties-of-the-engineer-manager-pendulum/)); or, in the advice-column format, the reader's own letter reproduced in full as a blockquote before she says a word. Headers are frequently the argument itself stated as a claim rather than a topic label — "Management is NOT a promotion," "Communication is not magic pixie dust," "cliches are a substitute for critical thinking" (note the lowercase "c," an unpolished, dashed-off texture she doesn't sand down). Bold is reserved for the single load-bearing sentence of a section, not for keyword skimming: "your job is to get better at management," "formal authority is the weakest form of power." Numbered lists carry sequential or ranked argument (ten reasons to be wary of the ladder, a ten-point cultural roadmap); bulleted lists carry parallel diagnostic items (deploy-fear symptoms, on-call recommendations). Footnotes are substantive, not citation stubs — they carry a caveat, a personal disclosure, or a second joke the main text couldn't hold ("Martin said I could tell this story and use his name... So if you're wondering if I'm talking about you: I'm not. Unless I asked first," [If Management Isn't A Promotion, Then Engineering Isn't A Demotion](https://charity.wtf/2020/09/06/if-management-isnt-a-promotion-then-engineering-isnt-a-demotion/)). She embeds screenshotted tweets and reaction images with declarative captions that function as running section dividers ("Deploys are the heartbeat of your company," "Good judgment matters more than rules"), a blog-era device distinct from prose proper. Pieces end on a first-name, lowercase sign-off — "charity." — sometimes with a heart, sometimes with a P.S. that adds one more concrete or tender beat, sometimes with a direct charge back to the reader's own agency ("I hope your team will leave you, and go find a place that truly values their time and sleep," [On Call Shouldn't Suck: A Guide For Managers](https://charity.wtf/2020/10/03/on-call-shouldnt-suck-a-guide-for-managers/)).

## Reference palette

Her metaphors are drawn overwhelmingly from bodies, animals, and violence-adjacent idiom used ironically to shock proportion into the reader: puppies being murdered, heart attacks versus diabetes, "catastrophuckes," "five-alarm fires," "PTSD-inducing outages." She reaches for cognitive and behavioral psychology as a legitimizing vocabulary for organizational dysfunction, naming specific named biases (prevention bias, zero-risk bias, the IKEA effect, hyperbolic discounting) rather than gesturing at "bias" in general, and cites empirical software-delivery research in passing (DORA, *Accelerate*) without building a sustained academic case around it. She borrows labor and class language to describe org dynamics — "engineering classism," "empire-builder," management as "overhead" — and occasionally reaches for constitutional/political structure as an organizing analogy ("much like the Constitution provides for Congress and the States, respectively," [If Management Isn't A Promotion, Then Engineering Isn't A Demotion](https://charity.wtf/2020/09/06/if-management-isnt-a-promotion-then-engineering-isnt-a-demotion/)). Startup and Twitter culture supply her in-jokes and shorthand (Kubernetes, Hacker News, "internet feminists," subtweeting). Because this corpus concentrates on management and deploys, it under-represents the technical-explainer register she uses in her observability and database writing — the palette above should be read as her essay/rant register, not her full range.

## Sentence-level habits

Short, blunt sentences do the punctuating work at the open and close of sections: "So, that happened." "Fix. That." "Barf. 🙄" "So go and LOOK." Longer sentences arrive as comma-chained cumulative lists that pile up examples of the same failure before naming it: "waiting until it is 'safe' to deploy, batching up changes into bigger changes that are decidedly unsafe to deploy, debugging broken deploys that had many changes batched into them, waiting nervously to get paged after a deploy goes out..." ([Friday Deploy Freezes Are Exactly Like Murdering Puppies](https://charity.wtf/2019/05/01/friday-deploy-freezes-are-exactly-like-murdering-puppies/)) Rhetorical questions stack in runs of three or four to build pressure before the answer lands: "What if you achieved the same thing with 10,000 people, instead? What would that say about your leadership? What if you achieved the same thing with 100 people? Or even 10 people?" ([Every Achievement Has A Denominator](https://charity.wtf/2022/10/03/every-achievement-has-a-denominator/)) ALL CAPS supplies vocal emphasis alongside — not instead of — bold: "YOUR COMPANY SUCKS," "HAAAAAATE," "EXACT SAME TIME." Ellipses mark comic trailing-off or a beat before a punchline: "I start to feel like I know what I'm doing (... a telltale sign something's wrong)." Parentheticals run as a second, quieter voice heckling the first one, mid-sentence. She interrupts her own drafting process on the page rather than hiding the seams: "(I originally titled this article 'Julian Dunn and the Case of the Bad Manager', lol)" ([If Management Isn't A Promotion, Then Engineering Isn't A Demotion](https://charity.wtf/2020/09/06/if-management-isnt-a-promotion-then-engineering-isnt-a-demotion/)). She signs every piece with her bare first name, lowercase — "charity." — never "Charity Majors," a small, consistent intimacy device. Semicolons are rare; she reaches for a period, a colon, or a comma run before a semicolon.

## Signature moves

1. **The tough-love letter.** Reproduce a reader's letter verbatim, respond "Dear [pseudonym]," in second person, braid validation with blunt confrontation, sign off "charity." Example: "Dear Cog?, Holy shitballs! What an amazing story! ... First of all, YOUR COMPANY SUCKS... Fuck em. Please leave." ([Questionable Advice: "After Being A Manager, Can I Be Happy As A Cog?"](https://charity.wtf/2019/11/23/questionable-advice-after-being-a-manager-can-i-be-happy-as-a-cog/))
2. **The bias glossary.** Build an entire post as a list of quoted bad-faith rationalizations, footnote each to a named psychological phenomenon, then append a full working glossary at the end so the reader can look each one up. Example: "'It is more dangerous to deploy than not to deploy' (prevention bias, zero-risk bias)" through two dozen more entries, followed by full definitions. ([Software Deploys and Cognitive Biases](https://charity.wtf/2021/08/27/software-deploys-and-cognitive-biases/))
3. **The tweetstorm-as-primary-source.** Instead of paraphrasing her own past public argument, re-embed the literal tweet inside the essay as the sentence carrying the thesis. Example: "treat every alarm like a heart attack. _fix_ the motherfucker. i do not care if this causes product development to screech to a halt." embedded as the encapsulating line of the on-call argument. ([On Call Shouldn't Suck: A Guide For Managers](https://charity.wtf/2020/10/03/on-call-shouldnt-suck-a-guide-for-managers/))
4. **The sparkle bracket.** Wrap a coined maxim in ✨ sparkle emoji — sometimes blown up into its own oversized header — to flag it as the post's quotable, shareable takeaway. Examples: "✨Every achievement has a denominator✨" ([Every Achievement Has A Denominator](https://charity.wtf/2022/10/03/every-achievement-has-a-denominator/)); the header sequence "You will be advised to stop writing code or engineering. ✨ Fuck That. ✨" ([Engineering Management: The Pendulum Or The Ladder](https://charity.wtf/2019/01/04/engineering-management-the-pendulum-or-the-ladder/))
5. **Serial callback.** Open a new post by explicitly cataloguing and linking her own prior posts on the same theme, building a visible canon in real time rather than assuming the reader remembers. Example: "A quick recap of the relevant posts: [The Engineer/Manager Pendulum] [Engineering Management: the Pendulum or the Ladder] [If Management Isn't A Promotion, Engineering Isn't A Demotion]." ([Twin Anxieties of the Engineer/Manager Pendulum](https://charity.wtf/2022/03/24/twin-anxieties-of-the-engineer-manager-pendulum/))
6. **The warm gut-punch coda.** End a profane, combative piece with an abrupt swing into tenderness — the first-name sign-off, a heart, or a P.S. that undercuts the fight with reassurance. Example: "P.S. — Oh and stop fretting about 'competing' with the 20-somethings kuberneteheads, you dork... The tech is the easy part. The tech will always be the easy part. 🙂" closing a letter that opened "YOUR COMPANY SUCKS." ([Questionable Advice: "After Being A Manager, Can I Be Happy As A Cog?"](https://charity.wtf/2019/11/23/questionable-advice-after-being-a-manager-can-i-be-happy-as-a-cog/))

## What NOT to do (what Charity never does)

- She never hedges a strong opinion into mush for the sake of appearing balanced — even when she disclaims dogmatism ("Ultimately, I am not dogmatic about Friday deploys. Truly, I'm not"), she immediately reasserts the position hard ("But call it and treat it like the hack it is," [Friday Deploy Freezes Are Exactly Like Murdering Puppies](https://charity.wtf/2019/05/01/friday-deploy-freezes-are-exactly-like-murdering-puppies/)).
- She never uses consultant buzzwords approvingly — "breaking down silos," "culture problem," and similar phrases appear only to be attacked, never adopted straight.
- She never bowdlerizes her own profanity for a "professional" register — no "eff," no "freaking," no asterisked-out letters, and no apology for it afterward ("pardon my French" does not appear in this corpus).
- She never builds a sustained, single extended metaphor carried paragraph-by-paragraph through an entire piece the way a literary essayist would; her images (puppies, heart attacks, denominators) are punchy and disposable, deployed once and dropped rather than developed.
- She never leaves an argument as pure vibes or pure feeling — every rant is paired with an operational checklist, numbered roadmap, or named-and-defined taxonomy immediately after the emotional case.
- She never resume-flexes at length; credentials appear in a clause in passing, not as a paragraph of throat-clearing.
- She rarely reaches for a semicolon; where a careful academic writer would use one to join two balanced clauses, she uses a period, a colon, or a comma run instead.
- She never writes in a detached third-person "the organization" register throughout a whole piece — she stays in first and second person even when describing systemic, org-wide problems.

## How this differs from adjacent writers

Against Will Larson (*An Elegant Puzzle*), whose register is systematized, calm, and rarely personal — frameworks built to be applied by someone else's judgment — Majors writes from her own gut and her own record, and expects the reader to feel confronted, not merely informed. Against Camille Fournier (*The Manager's Path*), whose voice is measured and almost therapeutic, Majors is combative first and comforting only in the coda; she will tell a reader their company sucks and to quit before she reassures them. Against Gergely Orosz (The Pragmatic Engineer), whose authority comes from reported data, surveys, and a journalistic remove from the subject, Majors builds arguments out of her own embedded tweets and her own lived management failures, staying inside the first person throughout. Against Lara Hogan's gentle, coaching-oriented empathy, Majors's empathy shows up as profanity on the reader's behalf ("Fuck em. Please leave") rather than as validating language — the tenderness is real, but it arrives dressed as a fight picked in your defense.

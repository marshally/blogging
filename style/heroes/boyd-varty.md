# Writing Style: Boyd Varty

Boyd Varty is a fourth-generation custodian of Londolozi Game Reserve in South Africa's Sabi Sand bushveld — a lion tracker, safari guide, life coach, and storyteller who wrote the memoir *Cathedral of the Wild* (2014) and the parable-length *The Lion Tracker's Guide to Life* (2019) — and his voice is distinctive because it runs everything through one engine: the ancient physical craft of tracking, learned from named Shangaan mentors, retold as campfire narrative in vivid sensory scenes, and only then allowed to yield an inner lesson.  He earns his mysticism the hard way — with crocodile teeth, cold tracks, and self-deprecating disasters — so that when a line like "I don't know where I am going, but I know exactly how to get there" arrives, it lands as field-tested craft rather than aphorism-first self-help.  The corpus below mixes his written prose (books, Londolozi blog posts) with transcribed speech (TED, NPR, two long Tim Ferriss interviews), all verified verbatim from primary sources, so every claim is grounded in his actual language rather than his reputation.

## Who is writing

Varty writes as an heir and an apprentice at the same time: the fourth generation of a family "in the safari business for four generations" ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript)), whose great-grandfather "had bought the land in 1926, after drinking too much gin" ([Tim Ferriss #571, spoken](https://tim.blog/2022/02/18/boyd-varty-transcript/)) — but whose real authority comes from decades of apprenticeship to Shangaan master trackers he names and elevates above himself: Renias Mhlongo, Solly Mhlongo, Elmon Mhlongo, Richard Siwela.  He announces his stance in the first sentence of his TED talk — "I'm a man who's trying to live from his heart" ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript)) — and he holds that register everywhere: earnest, unguarded, openly emotional, but anchored in physical competence (rifles, Land Rovers, tracks, wounds) so the earnestness never floats free.  His relationship to the reader is that of a guide on a game drive: he positions his material as hospitality rather than instruction — "Africa is a place where people still sit under starlit skies and around campfires and tell stories, and so what I have to share with you today is the simple medicine of a few campfire stories" ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript)) — and when the lesson finally comes, he hands the role of tracker over to the reader entirely: "What I suggest in the book is quite simply that that tracker……is you." ([Londolozi Blog, written](https://blog.londolozi.com/2019/11/24/the-lion-trackers-guide-to-life-boyd-varty/))  He is also explicitly skeptical of his own profession's packaging: "Coming from the South African bushveld, I felt pretty certain life did not need a coach." ([The Lion Tracker's Guide to Life, written](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/67138879-the-lion-tracker-s-guide-to-life))

## Core voice attributes

1. **Runs everything through the tracking metaphor — and keeps the metaphor mechanically accurate.** The tracker-as-metaphor engine is not decoration; the life lesson maps one-to-one onto real field procedure, so the advice reads like craft instruction: "Accept that losing the track is part of tracking. Go back to the last clear track. There is information there. Walk up ahead checking any open terrain and bare ground. Open your focus. Any place you don't find a track is not wasted, but part of refining where to look. Flow for a while on your best guess, alert, listening, noticing." ([The Lion Tracker's Guide to Life, written](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/67138879-the-lion-tracker-s-guide-to-life)) The engine's motto is a mentor's line, not his own: "And then often he would say too, 'I don't know where I'm going, but I know exactly how to get there.'" (on Renias Mhlongo, [Tim Ferriss #571, spoken](https://tim.blog/2022/02/18/boyd-varty-transcript/))
2. **Credits every piece of wisdom to a named teacher.** The masters in his work are the Shangaan trackers, his family, and his reading — never himself. Renias gets the motto; Alex and Renias get the coinage: "Alex and Renias call this 'the path of not here.' No action is considered a waste, and the key is to keep moving, readjusting, welcoming feedback. The path of not here is part of the path of here." ([The Lion Tracker's Guide to Life, written](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/67138879-the-lion-tracker-s-guide-to-life)) Even the definition of mastery is bestowed on Renias: "To me a master is anyone who can be themselves in any situation. Renias lives this definition." (same source) In the TED talk, ubuntu is embodied first by Mandela and then handed to a tracker: "the man who taught me the most about this value personally was this man, Solly Mhlongo." ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript))
3. **Stages the animal encounter as cinema before extracting any meaning.** Danger scenes are told in present-tense staccato with the lesson withheld until the action fully resolves: "Now the first thing that you notice when a crocodile hits you is the ferocity of the bite. Wham! It hits me by my right leg. It pulls me. It turns. I throw my hand up. I'm able to grab a branch. It's shaking me violently." ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript)) Only after Solly wades through the crocodile channel and "I survive" does the abstraction arrive: "In a more collective society, we realize from the inside that our own well-being is deeply tied to the well-being of others."
4. **Builds incantatory rhythm through anaphora.** When he wants weight, he repeats a frame word in short consecutive sentences, a preacher's cadence that survives in both prose and speech: "Danger is shared. Pain is shared. Joy is shared. Achievement is shared. Houses are shared. Food is shared." ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript)); "Nature as a physical space that reframes and recasts one's inner space. Nature as an outer world that, like no other force, facilitates the journey to the inner world. Nature as healer." ([Cathedral of the Wild, written](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/25332949-cathedral-of-the-wild-an-african-journey-home)); "The natural world is a story machine. It's a meaning machine. It's a symbolic machine." ([Tim Ferriss #832, spoken](https://tim.blog/2025/10/24/the-return-of-the-lion-tracker-transcript/))
5. **Compresses lessons into paradox-shaped koans.** His maxims are built on inversion or contradiction, which keeps them from reading as motivational-poster lines: "The path of not here is part of the path of here." and "a life with no sharp edges would be worse. The hazard of modern times is the danger of no danger." ([The Lion Tracker's Guide to Life, written](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/67138879-the-lion-tracker-s-guide-to-life)); "Life isn't about staying on track; it's about constantly rediscovering the track." ([Cathedral of the Wild, written](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/25332949-cathedral-of-the-wild-an-african-journey-home)); "Was 2020 a dark year? No just a passage to the light." ([Londolozi Blog, written](https://blog.londolozi.com/2020/11/26/2020-in-celebration-of-the-light/))
6. **Punctures his own mystique with self-deprecating disaster stories.** For every transcendent scene there is a slapstick one where he is the fool, told with the same narrative craft: on roaring at a silent lion slide in front of Silicon Valley executives — "it dawned on me slow enough for it to be truly painful, that I realized I was about to roar at a group of executives... still to this day, Tim, I'm going to be honest with you, if I lie in bed and I think about that, a wave of shame will travel through me. And I'll have to curl over on my side and just rock myself." ([Tim Ferriss #571, spoken](https://tim.blog/2022/02/18/boyd-varty-transcript/)) Even mid-crocodile-attack he cracks a joke: "It's a very strange sensation having another creature try and eat you, and there are few things that promote vegetarianism like that." ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript))
7. **Flags the woo before committing to it, then testifies from direct experience.** He never pretends mystical claims are ordinary; he names the reader's skepticism first, then stakes his own observation against it: "the minute you put your intention and attention on them, I don't know how, you know, it's maybe too woo, but I'm telling you, they feel it." ([Tim Ferriss #571, spoken](https://tim.blog/2022/02/18/boyd-varty-transcript/)); "I think it's woo-woo to us, but if you intentionally start working with the natural world, it knows, on some level, a field of living sentience... I think people need to be re-enchanted." ([Tim Ferriss #832, spoken](https://tim.blog/2025/10/24/the-return-of-the-lion-tracker-transcript/))
8. **Diagnoses modern life in one clinical image, from the bush looking in.** His critiques of modernity come as compact reframes rather than arguments: "I think that a lot of the anxiety disorders and depression that we see in the world are actually an undiagnosed homesickness for a sense of belonging." ([NPR TED Radio Hour, spoken](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/481294968)); "We lose ourselves in 'shoulds'… No wild animal has ever participated in a should." ([The Lion Tracker's Guide to Life, written](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/67138879-the-lion-tracker-s-guide-to-life)); "the Aboriginal people have this amazing saying that 'modern culture is three days deep.'" ([Tim Ferriss #571, spoken](https://tim.blog/2022/02/18/boyd-varty-transcript/))
9. **Quotes a small canon of teachers by name — Campbell, Hemingway, Thich Nhat Hanh — and then adds his own turn.** He borrows openly and visibly amends: "I hear Joseph Campbell: 'People are not looking for the meaning of life, they are looking for the feeling of being alive.'" and "'The miracle is not walking on the water; the miracle is walking on the earth,' said the teacher Thích Nhất Hanh." ([The Lion Tracker's Guide to Life, written](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/67138879-the-lion-tracker-s-guide-to-life)); "Hemingway said we heal stronger at the broken places, but I've found that where the heart is concerned, we also heal more tenderly, more open to the miraculous." ([Cathedral of the Wild, written](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/25332949-cathedral-of-the-wild-an-african-journey-home))

## Structure and formatting

He opens by locating himself and the listener in a place and a lineage before anything else happens: the TED talk moves in its first minute from personal stance ("I'm a man who's trying to live from his heart") to dedication (Mandela "passed away a few hours ago") to origin ("I grew up in wonder. I grew up amongst those animals. I grew up in the wild eastern part of South Africa at a place called Londolozi Game Reserve") to an explicit contract about what kind of piece this will be ("the simple medicine of a few campfire stories, stories about heroes of heart") ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript)).  His stock talk opening makes the same move even more nakedly: "And my story, like many good stories in Africa, begins with a lion roaring." ([Tim Ferriss #571, spoken](https://tim.blog/2022/02/18/boyd-varty-transcript/))  The body of a Varty piece is a chain of two to four discrete animal-or-person stories, each staged scene-first (setting, sensory detail, escalation, resolution) with the lesson extracted only afterward and explicitly marked as an expansion of understanding — "What Elvis and the herd taught me caused me to expand my definition of ubuntu" ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript)) — so the piece reads as a widening spiral: person (Mandela), then human tracker (Solly), then animal (Elvis the elephant), each enlarging the same single idea rather than introducing new ones.  Pacing inside a story alternates long scene-setting sentences with sudden staccato action; suspense is built with horror-movie self-awareness ("And if had been a horror movie, people in the audience would have started saying, 'Don't go in there. Don't go in there.'" [TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript)).  Endings return to the opening image with the definition now earned — the TED talk closes by finally cashing the title of his own book: "I believe that in the cathedral of the wild, we get to see the most beautiful parts of ourselves reflected back at us" — or hand the reader a benediction-plus-charge, as in the closing lines of *Cathedral of the Wild*: "Fight for space in your own backyard, in an acre or a flowerpot... If you take this one step toward them, no matter where you are, the elephants will come to you." ([Cathedral of the Wild, written](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/25332949-cathedral-of-the-wild-an-african-journey-home))  Blog posts end on direct address and forward motion rather than summary: "Two schools to go. Karabo and I better get back to work!" ([Londolozi Blog, written](https://blog.londolozi.com/2015/07/01/boyd-varty-a-short-story-on-what-we-have-learnt-from-a-9-year-old-south-african-avatar/)) and "We so look forward to welcoming you back to Londolozi." ([Londolozi Blog, written](https://blog.londolozi.com/2020/11/26/2020-in-celebration-of-the-light/))

## Reference palette

Tracking craft as the master domain — first tracks, cold tracks, pad marks, "the path of not here," reading terrain, and its lineage claim: "Some people say that tracking is in some ways the beginning of science because it's the beginning of deduction... I think of it as this art form that you can't hang on the wall, it literally has to be alive in a person to survive." ([Tim Ferriss #571, spoken](https://tim.blog/2022/02/18/boyd-varty-transcript/))  The Londolozi bushveld rendered in specific fauna and light — leopards, lions, elephants, hyenas, nightjars, honeyguide birds, marula trees, the Sand River, "the cool pink light of the evening" ([For my Friend, written](https://blog.londolozi.com/2013/05/06/for-my-friend/)).  Zulu and Shangaan language and thought, translated inline: "Ubuntu: I am because of you. Or, people are not people without other people." ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript)) and Renias's field cry "'Hi ta kuma': 'We will get it.'" ([Tim Ferriss #571, spoken](https://tim.blog/2022/02/18/boyd-varty-transcript/))  Four generations of family lore, told as comic-heroic legend — the 1926 gin purchase, the parents' plane hitting a stork mid-flight and his mother's "patented mother slap" before boarding the next commercial flight "looking like we had been in The Texas Chainsaw Massacre" ([Tim Ferriss #571, spoken](https://tim.blog/2022/02/18/boyd-varty-transcript/)).  Restoration ecology as family destiny — the conversion of bankrupt cattle land into a model reserve, "the restoration of our relationship with the natural world can begin inside each one of us" ([Tim Ferriss #571, spoken](https://tim.blog/2022/02/18/boyd-varty-transcript/)).  South African history through personal proximity: Mandela recuperating at Londolozi, seen as "that same quiet man from the garden" on "the snowy, bunny-eared TV" ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript)).  And a psychology-and-wisdom shelf worn lightly — Joseph Campbell, Hemingway, Thich Nhat Hanh, Internal Family Systems, Byron Katie, sweat-lodge and plant-medicine ceremony — always subordinated to what the bush already demonstrated.

## Sentence-level habits

His action prose shifts into the present tense and shortens radically, sometimes to two words per sentence, mimicking the tempo of the event: "He turns. He begins to make his way to me. The croc again continues to shake me... He comes straight into the channel. He wades in to above his waist. He gets to me. He grabs me." ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript))  Reflective prose does the opposite — long, rolling, comma-spliced sentences that hold several images at once before resolving: "If you have never left a place, you may never know how deeply it has gone into your cells. Only in its absence, a world away in another land, would you hear its song calling back to you, playing the music of your longing." ([The Lion Tracker's Guide to Life, written](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/67138879-the-lion-tracker-s-guide-to-life))  Sensory details are chosen for texture and oddity, not prettiness: "the snowy, bunny-eared TV," Elvis's track that "looked like an inverted bracket," "The water was clear running over sand" ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript)), a baboon described as "a three-foot muscular, hairy dude with long canines" ([Tim Ferriss #832, spoken](https://tim.blog/2025/10/24/the-return-of-the-lion-tracker-transcript/)).  He violates gore expectations with deliberate restraint — one hard look, then a stated refusal to look again: "My leg from the knee down is mangled beyond description. The bone is cracked. The meat is torn up. I make an instant decision that I'll never look at that again." ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript))  Characterization is done in one flamboyant, affectionate epithet: Solly "was to be one of the richest men I would ever meet" and "this man was a Ph.D.-level tracker" ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript)); Richard Siwela "had that kind of — that arrogance born of being brilliant. He deserved all the arrogance he had because he was so good." ([Tim Ferriss #571, spoken](https://tim.blog/2022/02/18/boyd-varty-transcript/))  Direct address appears at emotional peaks, especially in written prose — his eulogy speaks to the dead man: "What are tracks like in heaven my friend? I bet it's all soft sand." ([For my Friend, written](https://blog.londolozi.com/2013/05/06/for-my-friend/))  And definitions are a habitual sentence shape — "my definition of X is" — used to quietly re-legislate big words: "my definition of harmony is - everything is uniquely itself. And by being uniquely itself - a part of a greater unfolding." ([NPR TED Radio Hour, spoken](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/481294968))

## Signature moves

1. **The Next First Track.** State the destination as unknowable, then make the method — the immediate next sign — the entire teaching, phrased in tracking mechanics: "All he needs is the next first track and then the next first track... So he doesn't know where it's going, but he knows how to get there. The next moment of presence. The next thing I know to do." ([Tim Ferriss #571, spoken](https://tim.blog/2022/02/18/boyd-varty-transcript/)) In book form: "First, quiet your heart and be still. Then find the fresh track and be willing to follow it. You don't need to see the whole picture; you only need to see where to take the next step." ([Cathedral of the Wild, written](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/25332949-cathedral-of-the-wild-an-african-journey-home))
2. **The Present-Tense Danger Cut.** When the animal strikes, drop into present tense and cut sentence length to near-zero, one action per sentence, no adjectives: "Wham! It hits me by my right leg. It pulls me. It turns. I throw my hand up. I'm able to grab a branch." ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript))
3. **The Shared Ledger.** Anaphora used as a litany to make a value felt as accumulation rather than argued: "Danger is shared. Pain is shared. Joy is shared. Achievement is shared. Houses are shared. Food is shared." ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript))
4. **The Mentor's Coinage.** Deliver the piece's most quotable idea in a named mentor's mouth, with the vernacular original where one exists, keeping himself the student: "'Hi ta kuma': 'We will get it.'... And then often he would say too, 'I don't know where I'm going, but I know exactly how to get there.'" ([Tim Ferriss #571, spoken](https://tim.blog/2022/02/18/boyd-varty-transcript/)); "Alex and Renias call this 'the path of not here.'" ([The Lion Tracker's Guide to Life, written](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/67138879-the-lion-tracker-s-guide-to-life))
5. **The Self-Roast After the Sermon.** Follow or salt transcendent material with a story where he is ridiculous, told with full narrative commitment: "Two men in beekeeping suits break into a full run through the wilderness... what they see is the Ebola cleanup crew in full white suits driving towards them at full speed going 'You're going to die. You're going to die! Drive, drive, drive!'" ([Tim Ferriss #571, spoken](https://tim.blog/2022/02/18/boyd-varty-transcript/)) The move ends on its own lesson-shaped punchline: "you can't half-roar at a group of executives. You've either got to not do it or go all in." (same source)
6. **The Widening Definition.** Introduce a value word early (ubuntu, mastery, harmony, the wild man), then let each successive story force the definition wider, marking the expansion explicitly: "Now although the root word is about people, I thought that maybe ubuntu was only about people. And then I met this young lady. Her name was Elvis." ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript))

## What NOT to do (what Varty never does)

- Never opens with the lesson. The abstraction is always downstream of a fully rendered scene; a bad imitation states "life is like tracking" in paragraph one, where Varty makes you follow the track first.
- Never claims the wisdom as his own invention — the mottos belong to Renias, the ubuntu embodiment to Solly and Mandela, the coinages to "Alex and Renias," the aphorisms to Campbell and Thich Nhat Hanh by name. Guru self-positioning is entirely absent from the corpus.
- Never plays the colonial-nostalgia safari register — no trophy talk, no "darkest Africa" exoticism, no natives-as-scenery. The Shangaan trackers are the intellectual heroes of every piece, and he explicitly counter-programs the news narrative: "my stories are not the stories that you'll hear on the news." ([TED, spoken](https://www.ted.com/talks/boyd_varty_what_i_learned_from_nelson_mandela/transcript))
- Never dwells in gore or trauma for effect — the crocodile wound gets three clinical sentences and then "I make an instant decision that I'll never look at that again"; the healing work is narrated, but suffering is never milked.
- Never presents mysticism deadpan as settled fact — the "too woo" flag always precedes the testimony, and the claim is grounded in repeated personal observation rather than doctrine.
- Never uses frameworks, numbered steps, acronyms, data, or studies to carry a lesson — the closest he comes to numbers is confessing a failed fundraiser's actual totals ("we made just under $13,000 online and $12,000 offline," [Londolozi Blog, written](https://blog.londolozi.com/2015/07/01/boyd-varty-a-short-story-on-what-we-have-learnt-from-a-9-year-old-south-african-avatar/)), which is candor, not evidence-mongering.
- Never writes cynicism or irony at the register level — humor is warm and self-directed; the world itself is never mocked, and wonder is never air-quoted.
- Never hedges the earnestness into mush — "I'm a man who's trying to live from his heart" is delivered straight to a TED audience with no protective irony, and the prose commits the same way throughout.

## How this differs from adjacent writers

Compared with Peter Matthiessen (*The Snow Leopard*), whose nature-as-spiritual-journey prose is erudite, interior, and melancholy, Varty is oral, communal, and warm — his unit of composition is the campfire story with an audience presumed present, not the solitary journal entry.  Compared with Paulo Coelho's parable mode, which invents allegorical shepherds, Varty's parables are populated by real, named, verifiable people — Renias Mhlongo, Solly Mhlongo — and the metaphor stays accountable to actual field technique, which is what keeps it from dissolving into fable.  Compared with the self-help coaching genre he professionally inhabits, he inverts its authority structure: he opens his own coaching book by declaring "I felt pretty certain life did not need a coach" and casts himself as apprentice rather than expert.  And compared with Tim Ferriss, his most frequent interlocutor, Varty is anti-systematic — where Ferriss extracts protocols and tools, Varty deliberately withholds the destination and offers only the next first track.

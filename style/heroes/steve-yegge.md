# Writing Style: Steve Yegge

Steve Yegge is a career backend/systems engineer (Geoworks, Amazon, Google, Grab, Sourcegraph) who has spent over twenty years writing enormous, discursive, first-person essays about programming, hiring, language design, and corporate dysfunction — first as "Stevey's Drunken Blog Rants" on an internal Amazon wiki, then "Stevey's Blog Rants" on Blogger during his Google years, then in occasional Medium posts after he left Google. The voice is distinctive because it refuses every convention of professional tech writing at once: posts run 3,000–6,000+ words, open with a joke or a personal anecdote rather than a thesis, digress for paragraphs at a time, use profanity and self-deprecation as argumentative tools, and routinely tell the reader outright that the writer is drunk, tired, or has been putting this off for years. The result reads like a smart, funny, slightly unhinged senior engineer holding court at a bar — except the argument underneath is usually rigorous and the digressions are load-bearing, not filler.

## Corpus analyzed

1. [Stevey's Google Platforms Rant](https://gist.github.com/chitchcock/1281611) (Oct 2011, leaked from Google+; [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=3101876)) — his single most consequential post: an internal-turned-viral critique of Google's failure to build platforms, credited with reshaping how the industry talks about "platform vs. product."
2. [Execution in the Kingdom of Nouns](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2006/03/execution-in-kingdom-of-nouns.html) (Mar 2006) — the canonical Yegge piece, an extended fairy-tale parable mocking Java's noun-obsession; still cited as a classic critique of OO verbosity two decades later.
3. [Good Agile, Bad Agile](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2006/09/good-agile-bad-agile_27.html) (Sep 2006) — one of the earliest and most widely discussed contrarian takes on the Agile movement, still referenced in Agile-skepticism circles.
4. [Get That Job at Google](https://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/03/get-that-job-at-google.html) (Mar 2008) — the definitive tech-interview-prep essay of its era, spawning an entire genre of "how to pass a FAANG interview" writing.
5. [You Should Write Blogs](https://sites.google.com/site/steveyegge2/you-should-write-blogs) (Jan 2005, Amazon-era) — his meta-essay on why engineers should blog; frequently recirculated (e.g., resurfaced on Hacker News in 2017) as evergreen advice.
6. [Rich Programmer Food](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2007/06/rich-programmer-food.html) (Jun 2007) — the widely quoted argument that programmers should study compilers, cited constantly in "what CS topics actually matter" debates.
7. [Programming's Dirtiest Little Secret](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/09/programmings-dirtiest-little-secret.html) (Sep 2008) — his touch-typing polemic, notable as a masterclass in stretching a small practical tip into a full narrative essay.
8. [Notes from the Mystery Machine Bus](https://gist.github.com/mrnugget/49ad3ee4043c746e42187e2820ddc2f6) (Aug 2012, originally Google+; [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=4365255)) — his "conservative vs. liberal" taxonomy of software engineers, still cited as a framework for language/culture debates.
9. [Practicing Programming](https://sites.google.com/site/steveyegge2/practicing-programming) (2005, Amazon-era) — an early, quieter essay on deliberate practice for programmers, notable for prefiguring the "10,000 hours" conversation years before it was mainstream.
10. [Dear Google Cloud: Your Deprecation Policy is Killing You](https://steve-yegge.medium.com/dear-google-cloud-your-deprecation-policy-is-killing-you-ee7525dc05dc) (Aug 2020, Medium) — his most-discussed post-Google piece, widely covered (Hacker News, Last Week in AWS podcast) as a landmark public critique of GCP's deprecation culture.

## Who is writing

The persona is a long-tenured, opinionated staff-level engineer who has worked inside the machine (Amazon, Google) and is willing to say what colleagues only say in private. He writes as an insider settling scores and correcting the record, not as an outside pundit — his authority comes from having been in the room, not from credentials he lists. He treats the reader as a peer and equal, addressing them directly and often in imagined dialogue ("**Me:**... **You:**...") rather than lecturing from above. He is unashamed of being unpolished: posts open by announcing he's drunk, tired, or has avoided the topic for years, and he treats those admissions as part of the argument's credibility rather than something to hide. Across fifteen-plus years the persona is remarkably stable — profane, funny, digressive, self-mocking — but its intensity flexes with venue: the Amazon-internal pieces (You Should Write Blogs, Practicing Programming) are gentler and nearly profanity-free, written to encourage colleagues, while the public Google-era rants and the 2020 Medium piece let the profanity and contempt run much hotter, aimed at institutions rather than people.

## Core voice attributes

1. **Extended, sustained metaphor as argument, not decoration.** Where a lesser writer would use one analogy per paragraph, Yegge builds an entire piece around a single conceit and runs it to its logical (and illogical) extremes. Kingdom of Nouns doesn't just call Java noun-obsessed — it stages an entire fairy-tale kingdom with a king, oppressed verb-serfs, and a parody nursery rhyme rewritten in Java pseudocode ("For the lack of a nail, throw new HorseshoeNailNotFoundException(\"no nails!\");" — [Execution in the Kingdom of Nouns](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2006/03/execution-in-kingdom-of-nouns.html)). Good Agile, Bad Agile opens on cholesterol and rat poison and keeps threading "Good Kind" vs. "Bad Kind" through the whole essay.

2. **Confessional, first-person vulnerability as a rhetorical weapon.** He routinely undercuts his own authority before the reader can: "Guess what grade I got? I got a zero. As in, 0.0." ([Rich Programmer Food](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2007/06/rich-programmer-food.html)); "I kinda didn't make it in on my first attempt, but I begged like a street dog until they gave me a second round of interviews" ([Get That Job at Google](https://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/03/get-that-job-at-google.html)). The admission of failure or drunkenness makes the subsequent argument feel earned rather than lectured.

3. **The imagined dialogue.** He stages arguments as a script between himself and an imaginary reader, letting the reader's objections escalate to absurdity before answering them: "**You:** Should I work at Google?... **Me:** Yes. **You:** To which ques... wait, what do you mean by 'Yes?'... **Me:** Dude, the answer is Yes already, OK? It's an invariant." ([Get That Job at Google](https://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/03/get-that-job-at-google.html)). This lets him pre-empt every objection without sounding defensive.

4. **Profanity calibrated to venue and target, never to the reader.** The Amazon-era and hiring pieces are nearly clean; the institutional take-downs let loose. "Google, wake the fuck up. It's 2020. You are still losing." ([Dear Google Cloud](https://steve-yegge.medium.com/dear-google-cloud-your-deprecation-policy-is-killing-you-ee7525dc05dc)); "They don't give a single shit about charity or helping the needy" ([Platforms Rant](https://gist.github.com/chitchcock/1281611)). Profanity is always aimed at institutions, policies, or his own past self — never at the reader or at named individuals doing their jobs in good faith.

5. **A real, specific, often shaggy-dog anecdote earns the polemic before the polemic starts.** Dear Google Cloud spends nearly a third of its length on the Bigtable-codelab-forgotten-for-two-years story before pivoting to the deprecation argument it's actually about — the anecdote isn't color, it's the evidence that Google's engineering culture is genuinely excellent, which is what makes the later betrayal land. "I thanked them and shut it down and life went on. But I still think about that letter, thirteen years later." ([Dear Google Cloud](https://steve-yegge.medium.com/dear-google-cloud-your-deprecation-policy-is-killing-you-ee7525dc05dc))

6. **Numbered manifestos and mock-bureaucratic lists deployed for satire.** Bezos's "Big Mandate" in the Platforms Rant is rendered as a numbered corporate edict ending in a fake item: "7) Thank you; have a nice day!" followed immediately by "Ha, ha! ... #7 was a little joke I threw in, because Bezos most definitely does not give a shit about your day." ([Platforms Rant](https://gist.github.com/chitchcock/1281611)) The form of officialdom is used against itself.

7. **Meta-commentary on the act of writing the post, live, inside the post.** He narrates his own bar-progress and stalling in real time: "I see that I'm on my third *(Editor's Note: fourth)* glass of wine, which means my typing is about to give out any minute." ([Notes from the Mystery Machine Bus](https://gist.github.com/mrnugget/49ad3ee4043c746e42187e2820ddc2f6)) "I honestly don't know how to wrap this up. I've said pretty much everything I came here to say today." ([Platforms Rant](https://gist.github.com/chitchcock/1281611))

## Structure and formatting

- **Opens on a hook, never a thesis.** A joke ("Hey man, I don't know that stuff / Stevey's talking aboooooout" — [Get That Job at Google](https://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/03/get-that-job-at-google.html)), a personal anecdote, an analogy (cholesterol, Olive Garden), or a flat confession of reluctance ("God dammit, I didn't want to blog again." — [Dear Google Cloud](https://steve-yegge.medium.com/dear-google-cloud-your-deprecation-policy-is-killing-you-ee7525dc05dc)). The thesis, if stated at all, arrives paragraphs or pages later.
- **Epigraphs from unexpected sources.** A quoted line sits atop several posts before the prose starts: Humpty Dumpty on verbs ([Kingdom of Nouns](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2006/03/execution-in-kingdom-of-nouns.html)), a Wikipedia line on rugby scrums ([Good Agile, Bad Agile](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2006/09/good-agile-bad-agile_27.html)), Reservoir Dogs' Mr. Pink on typing ([Programming's Dirtiest Little Secret](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/09/programmings-dirtiest-little-secret.html)), his own brother Dave ("The Olive Garden: it's where poor people go to eat rich people food." — [Rich Programmer Food](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2007/06/rich-programmer-food.html)).
- **Very long by any blogging standard.** Most pieces in this corpus run well past 2,500 words; Dear Google Cloud runs to a 23-minute read per Medium's own estimate, and the Platforms Rant, Kingdom of Nouns, and Mystery Machine Bus are all multi-thousand-word single sittings with no natural stopping point until the end.
- **Digression is structural, not incidental.** Tangents about his brother, his college transcript, or a random party anecdote aren't asides to be skipped — they carry the argument's emotional weight, and cutting them would flatten the piece into a listicle.
- **Bolded section headers as mini-chapter titles**, often playful rather than descriptive: "**Cots and Beards**," "**The Garbage Overfloweth**," "**Emergent Properties versus The Whip**," "**Learning Licks**." They announce a bit or a story, not a topic sentence.
- **Numbered lists used two ways**: as satirical mock-bureaucracy (Bezos's seven-item mandate) and as genuinely useful, concrete drills ("**Practice Drill #1:** Write your resume..." through Drill #12 in [Practicing Programming](https://sites.google.com/site/steveyegge2/practicing-programming)).
- **Endings decline to resolve cleanly.** He frequently admits he doesn't know how to stop: "I honestly don't know how to wrap this up" ([Platforms Rant](https://gist.github.com/chitchcock/1281611)); "I hope this was helpful. Let the flames begin, etc. Yawn." ([Get That Job at Google](https://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/03/get-that-job-at-google.html)); "That's it. That's my article." ([Programming's Dirtiest Little Secret](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/09/programmings-dirtiest-little-secret.html)). The Medium-era piece appends a running italicized postscript of corrections and updates after the "real" ending, treating the post as a living document rather than a sealed artifact.
- **Register shifts by venue.** The 2005 Amazon-internal essays (You Should Write Blogs, Practicing Programming) are gentle, encouraging, and nearly profanity-free, written for colleagues he wants to help. The public Blogger-era rants (2006–2008) are funnier and more theatrical. The 2011–2012 Google+ posts are the longest and most structurally ambitious (fairy tales, political taxonomies). The 2020 Medium piece is the angriest and most profane of the corpus — the same voice, turned up because the audience and the target both changed.

## Reference palette

Compilers and CS fundamentals (parsing, type systems, garbage collection), classic and pop literature (Kurt Vonnegut, Stephen King's *On Writing*, Humpty Dumpty/*Through the Looking-Glass*), fairy tales and fables (Goldilocks, "For Want of a Nail," Grail Quest), 1990s film (*Reservoir Dogs*, implicitly *Fight Club* via the Tyler Durden riff), American football and the NFL, real-world politics (conservative/liberal as a taxonomy), corporate satire (Scientology-as-consulting-scam origin story, mock HR memos), and heavy use of his own family and biography (his brother Dave, his own childhood, his University of Washington transcript) as primary source material rather than invented illustration. The palette is personal and idiosyncratic rather than curated for range — the same handful of touchstones (Vonnegut, football, his brother, his own failures) recur across posts written years apart.

## Sentence-level habits

- **Extreme sentence-length variance for comic timing.** A long, breathless, comma-spliced sentence is routinely followed by a two-word fragment: "You know. Chumps. That's the word I'm looking for." ([Good Agile, Bad Agile](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2006/09/good-agile-bad-agile_27.html))
- **Staccato one-word-per-sentence emphasis.** "The. Most. Important. Thing." ([Platforms Rant](https://gist.github.com/chitchcock/1281611)) — periods inserted mid-phrase purely for weight.
- **Direct second-person address throughout**, often as "Dude" regardless of the reader's actual identity: "Dude, the answer is Yes already, OK?" / "You may be a woman, but I'm still calling you Dude." ([Get That Job at Google](https://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/03/get-that-job-at-google.html))
- **Self-interrupting asides in dashes and parentheses**, frequently confessional: "(drinking, that is, not sawing my leg off)" ([Rich Programmer Food](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2007/06/rich-programmer-food.html)); "(Note: I Do Not Speak For My Employer... When employers want someone to speak for them, they hire a person like the Mouth of Sauron...)" ([Notes from the Mystery Machine Bus](https://gist.github.com/mrnugget/49ad3ee4043c746e42187e2820ddc2f6)).
- **Repetition-as-emphasis, spelled out rather than italicized:** "That was 15 years ago! 15! 15! 15!" ([Programming's Dirtiest Little Secret](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/09/programmings-dirtiest-little-secret.html)); "Even if you already think they're important, it's probably more than you think." ([Get That Job at Google](https://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/03/get-that-job-at-google.html))
- **Rhetorical questions that assume the reader's objection and voice it for them:** "Wouldn't it be strange if we suddenly decided that we could no longer use verbs?" ([Kingdom of Nouns](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2006/03/execution-in-kingdom-of-nouns.html)); "You wouldn't really think that an online bookstore needs to be an extensible, programmable platform. Would you?" ([Platforms Rant](https://gist.github.com/chitchcock/1281611))
- **Deliberately colloquial, phonetically-spelled speech**, especially in imagined dialogue and songs: "Stevey's talking aboooooout... I'm gonna get fiiiiiiiiiired... Oooh yeah baaaby baaaay-beeeeee" ([Get That Job at Google](https://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/03/get-that-job-at-google.html)).
- **Understated, throwaway kickers on serious claims**: "I sure hope they fix this, so I can take the trash out and get back to my video game. Or whatever I was doing." ([Kingdom of Nouns](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2006/03/execution-in-kingdom-of-nouns.html))

## Signature moves

1. **The extended fairy-tale/parable conceit.** An entire technical argument is restaged as a fictional kingdom, story, or parody nursery rhyme, run to its full absurd conclusion rather than dropped after one paragraph. Example: the Java "For Want of a Nail" parody rendered entirely in fake enterprise Java class names ("`BattleNotificationSender`," "`MessageTransportType.MOUNTED_RIDER`") — [Execution in the Kingdom of Nouns](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2006/03/execution-in-kingdom-of-nouns.html).

2. **Fictionalized third-person self-insertion.** He tells true autobiographical stories about himself in the third person under an invented, faintly ridiculous name, framed explicitly as fiction even though it's fact: "Once upon a time in '82, there was this completely hypothetical fictitious made-up dorky 12-year-old kid named Yeev Staigey, who was enduring his sophomore year at Paradise High School in Paradise, California." ([Programming's Dirtiest Little Secret](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/09/programmings-dirtiest-little-secret.html))

3. **The translated-subtext letter.** He quotes an actual polite, corporate document verbatim, then immediately supplies a second version "translating" its real meaning — usually in profane, blunt terms. He does this with a Google Cloud deprecation email ("_Dear Google Cloud Platform User..._" followed by "_Fuck yooooouuuuuuuu. Fuck you, fuck you, Fuck You..._" — [Dear Google Cloud](https://steve-yegge.medium.com/dear-google-cloud-your-deprecation-policy-is-killing-you-ee7525dc05dc)) and, structurally, with the imagined interview-candidate freakout in [Get That Job at Google](https://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/03/get-that-job-at-google.html).

4. **The mock-clinical diagnosis.** Real companies get a fake DSM-style diagnosis line, delivered totally deadpan: "**Microsoft** -- *Diagnosis: Batshit Conservative.*" ([Notes from the Mystery Machine Bus](https://gist.github.com/mrnugget/49ad3ee4043c746e42187e2820ddc2f6))

5. **Live meta-narration of the writing process as part of the piece.** He tracks his own drinking, wine count, and stamina inside the essay itself, and treats the difficulty of ending the post as content: "But that's just my take on it, and it's 4:00am." ([Good Agile, Bad Agile](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2006/09/good-agile-bad-agile_27.html)); "I see that I'm on my third *(Editor's Note: fourth)* glass of wine..." ([Notes from the Mystery Machine Bus](https://gist.github.com/mrnugget/49ad3ee4043c746e42187e2820ddc2f6))

6. **The imagined Q&A dialogue.** Entire sections are staged as a scripted back-and-forth between "**Me:**" and "**You:**," letting the imagined reader's anxiety escalate to comic extremity before being answered — see the interviewing panic spiral and the "Should I work at Google?" exchange, both in [Get That Job at Google](https://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2008/03/get-that-job-at-google.html).

## What NOT to do (what Yegge never does)

- **Never opens with a thesis statement, an abstract, or a "TL;DR" up top** (Mystery Machine Bus's TL;DR arrives after a full page of preamble, not before it) — a bad imitation would front-load the argument for skimmability.
- **Never keeps a post short.** There is no tweet-length or 500-word Yegge post in this corpus; brevity is not a value he optimizes for, and a bad imitation clipped down to "digestible" length would lose the entire persona.
- **Never hedges an opinion into mush.** He states sweeping claims flatly ("Amazon does everything wrong, and Google does everything right") and only qualifies afterward, if at all — never softens the claim itself with "in my opinion, at least in some cases."
- **Never uses profanity as decoration or a tic.** Every instance in this corpus lands on an institution, a policy, or his own past incompetence — never scattered in as filler or aimed at the reader.
- **Never writes in a corporate, hedge-everything, LinkedIn-safe register**, even when explicitly disclaiming that he doesn't speak for his employer — the disclaimer itself is delivered as a joke ("Hopefully it's more 'birthday surprised' than 'rhino startled in the wild' surprised" — [Good Agile, Bad Agile](http://steve-yegge.blogspot.com/2006/09/good-agile-bad-agile_27.html)), not as boilerplate.
- **Never uses invented or composite anecdotes for effect.** Every extended story in this corpus is presented as something that actually happened to him or someone he knows by name (his brother Dave, Ron Braunstein, the Bigtable team) — even the "Yeev Staigey" fairy-tale framing is explicitly true underneath its fictional wrapper, not embellished.
- **Never resolves a piece with a tidy, motivational bow.** Endings admit uncertainty, trail into a joke, or explicitly say the writer doesn't know how to stop — a bad imitation would add a confident summary paragraph that this corpus never provides.
- **Never uses SEO-style structure**: no numbered "Top 10" framing devices for their own sake, no keyword-stuffed headers, no listicle scaffolding imposed on material that wants to be read start to finish.

## How this differs from adjacent writers

Compared to Paul Graham, Yegge is far longer, messier, and more willing to let a digression run for its own sake rather than in service of an aphorism — Graham polishes toward a quotable maxim, Yegge builds toward a shaggy-dog anecdote that only pays off pages later. Compared to Joel Spolsky, Yegge is rowdier and more personally vulnerable; Spolsky's business logic stays crisp and buttoned-up even when opinionated, while Yegge routinely narrates his own drinking, failures, and doubt as part of the argument. Compared to Jeff Atwood (Coding Horror), Yegge is much less curatorial and much more confessional — Atwood synthesizes other people's research and links heavily, where Yegge argues almost entirely from his own institutional memory and personal history. The throughline that separates Yegge from all three: he treats the blog post itself as a performance with a persona in real time (drunk, tired, procrastinating, changing his mind mid-paragraph), not as a polished essay assembled after the fact.

# Writing Style: Simon Wardley

Simon Wardley is the creator of Wardley Mapping, a technique for visualizing business landscapes on two axes — visibility to the user and evolutionary maturity — developed while he was CEO of a UK photo and cloud-computing startup called Fotango, later refined as a researcher for CSC/DXC's Leading Edge Forum, and given away in full under Creative Commons on his blog (blog.gardeviance.org, "Bits or pieces?") and in a free, unfinished book, *Wardley Maps*, serialized chapter by chapter on Medium under the publication @wardleymaps. The voice is distinctive in the strategy-writing genre because it refuses the consultant's register almost entirely: instead of prescribing a framework from a position of authority, Wardley narrates his own decade-long confusion as a "fake CEO" who didn't understand strategy, builds every argument out of a hand-drawn diagram rather than prose alone, and scaffolds the whole system on Sun Tzu's five factors and John Boyd's OODA loop rather than on business-school vocabulary. The result reads like a self-deprecating British engineer's field notebook that happens to have reshaped how a generation of technologists think about strategy.

## Corpus analyzed

1. [On being lost](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/on-being-lost-2ef5f05eb1ec) (Chapter 1 of *Wardley Maps*, Medium, Aug 2016) — the book's origin chapter and the canonical statement of the "imposter CEO" narrative; the piece every reader of the book encounters first and the one most excerpted in talks and secondary write-ups of Wardley's biography.
2. [Finding a path](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/finding-a-path-cdb1249078c0) (Chapter 2, Medium, Aug 2016) — introduces the map itself (evolution axis, value chain, the Thomas Thwaites toaster) and is the piece most directly cited whenever someone explains "what a Wardley map actually is."
3. [Doctrine](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2016/08/doctrine.html) (Chapter 4, also on [Medium](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/doctrine-8bb0015688e5), Aug 2016) — the fullest exposition of his "universal principles" list, routinely linked as the reference chapter for doctrine in Wardley Mapping communities and conference talks.
4. [The play and a decision to act](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/the-play-and-a-decision-to-act-8eb796b1dff1) (Chapter 5, Medium, Aug 2016) — the Zimki/serverless-before-serverless case study; the chapter most cited as proof the method predates the technology it later explained, and the clearest example of the military-scaffolded "gameplay" vocabulary.
5. [Anticipation](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2016/12/anticipation.html) (Chapter 9, blog version, Dec 2016) — introduces the peace/war/wonder climatic cycle and the p(what)/p(when)/p(who) predictability framework, the piece most often linked when people ask how Wardley Mapping claims to "predict" anything.
6. [Better for Less](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2017/08/better-for-less.html) (Chapter 18, blog, Aug 2017) — his account of co-writing the real 2010 UK government IT reform paper of the same name; the clearest fusion of class self-deprecation, government-failure bluntness, and a catalogue of named cognitive biases, and a piece he flags himself as "a very rough draft."
7. [An introduction to Wardley (Value Chain) Mapping](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2015/02/an-introduction-to-wardley-value-chain.html) (blog, Feb 2015) — appears on his own blog's "Popular Posts" sidebar and was independently republished by CIO.com; the standalone, numbered ten-step tutorial that most non-book readers encounter first.
8. [Why the fuss about serverless?](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2016/11/why-fuss-about-serverless.html) (blog, Nov 2016) — his best-known single-topic technology essay, also delivered as a recurring conference talk (GOTO, iJS) for years afterward, and the piece that popularized "FinDev" and the co-evolution argument for serverless.
9. [On Pioneers, Settlers, Town Planners and Theft](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2015/03/on-pioneers-settlers-town-planners-and.html) (blog, Mar 2015) — also on the blog's own "Popular Posts" list; the fullest standalone statement of the PST organizational model that has since become industry shorthand (later relabeled Explorer/Villager/Town Planner for its colonial connotations).
10. [How to master strategy as simply as I can](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2016/11/how-to-master-strategy-as-simply-as-i.html) (blog, Nov 2016) — his own compressed, numbered-steps synthesis of the entire strategy cycle in under 900 words, frequently linked as the single-page summary of the whole method.

## Who is writing

Wardley writes as a working practitioner recounting his own field notebook, not as a consultant delivering a framework from outside. The entire book opens with him admitting he was an "imposter CEO" who, handed a strategy document in a hotel lift two decades earlier, "hadn't a clue whether it did or not" and answered "seems fine to me" purely because the words looked familiar ([On being lost](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/on-being-lost-2ef5f05eb1ec)). His authority comes entirely from having run the company, made the mistakes, and lived with the consequences — not from a credential or a hired vantage point. He explicitly refuses the guru posture: "I don't claim to have mystical powers of anticipation, a time machine, some great intellect or a crystal ball... I'm a lousy prognosticator and a very normal sort of person" ([Anticipation](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2016/12/anticipation.html)). His relationship to the reader is that of a fellow struggler handing over a tool he wishes someone had given him: "I dearly wish I had known these techniques at the beginning... So in the forlorn hope of helping the reader... I've written a short guide to those practices" ([An introduction to Wardley (Value Chain) Mapping](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2015/02/an-introduction-to-wardley-value-chain.html)). He positions himself against the entire apparatus of orthodox business strategy — SWOTs, 2x2s, Porter's forces, business school "secrets of success" books — not as tools that are merely imperfect but as tools that actively prevented him from seeing what mattered: "I started using 2x2s, SWOTS, Porter's forces and all manner of instruments. Everything felt lacking, nothing satisfied" ([On being lost](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/on-being-lost-2ef5f05eb1ec)). Where a consultant writes to be hired, Wardley writes as someone giving the method away for free under Creative Commons because he was helped by no one and wants the next confused executive to have what he didn't.

## Core voice attributes

1. **The confessional discovery narrative.** Wardley never states a conclusion cold; he re-lives the specific, dated moment he was ignorant of it, then walks the reader through his own realization in real time. "A decade later, I had risen through the ranks to become the CEO of another company... I still had no means to understand what a good strategy was... I was an imposter CEO! I needed to learn fast before anyone found out. But how?" ([On being lost](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/on-being-lost-2ef5f05eb1ec)). The technique is never introduced as a claim to be evaluated; it's introduced as the thing that rescued him from a specific, embarrassing state.
2. **Bluntness about his own failure, undiluted by hedging.** When something went wrong, he names himself as the cause without softening it. "The problem was me. I had massively underestimated the intentions of the parent company... Talk about a rookie mistake. I had finally been rumbled as that imposter CEO" ([The play and a decision to act](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/the-play-and-a-decision-to-act-8eb796b1dff1)). The Zimki chapter — the story of building the first public PaaS two years before Google App Engine and then losing the company over it — ends not with a lesson dressed up as triumph but with plain ownership: "It was my job to lead the company and that didn't just mean those who worked for me but also the board."
3. **Class- and background-conscious self-deprecation.** His self-deprecation isn't generic false modesty; it's specifically rooted in class and outsider status, especially when he's in rooms with institutional power. "What on earth was I, a state school kid who had lived on a council estate doing in the Houses of Parliament talking to people I'd seen on TV" ([Better for Less](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2017/08/better-for-less.html)). Even his stated purpose can be undercut with dry contempt: describing his own company's mission statement, "It sucked. It was actually a botch job" ([On being lost](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/on-being-lost-2ef5f05eb1ec)).
4. **The diagram as the argument, not an illustration of it.** He builds the case by making the reader look at two competing pictures side by side and asking them to judge, rather than asserting a verdict. After placing a real map of the battle of Thermopylae next to a SWOT diagram of the same battle: "Now, ask yourself, what do you think would be more effective in combat — a strategy built upon an understanding of the landscape or a SWOT diagram?... Which was I using in running my business — a map or a SWOT? The wrong one" ([On being lost](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/on-being-lost-2ef5f05eb1ec)).
5. **Military and Sun Tzu scaffolding treated as literal structure, not decoration.** He doesn't reach for Sun Tzu as a quotable epigraph; he adopts the five factors (purpose, landscape, climate, doctrine, leadership) as the actual organizing skeleton of the whole book. "Sun Tzu had described five factors that matter in competition between two opponents. Loosely speaking, these are: — purpose, landscape, climate, doctrine and leadership. I've drawn them as a circle in figure 1" ([On being lost](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/on-being-lost-2ef5f05eb1ec)). Boyd's OODA loop gets the same treatment: "The best way I've found to cope with this cycle is through the work of the mad major himself — the exceptional John Boyd... I've married together both Sun Tzu and John Boyd to create a strategy cycle" (same source).
6. **Refusal to claim the method is magic or complete.** He repeatedly undercuts his own tool's authority in the same breath he's teaching it. "Remember, all models are wrong but some are useful" ([On being lost](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/on-being-lost-2ef5f05eb1ec)); "There are no simple answers with maps... all maps are imperfect and they are nothing more than an aid to learning and communication" ([Better for Less](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2017/08/better-for-less.html)); "Maps aren't a 2x2 where your goal is to get into some corner to win the magic prize" (same source).
7. **Named, itemized taxonomies as evidence, not just organization.** When a concept has variants, he doesn't gesture at "various biases" — he lists and defines each one by name, admitting the list itself is biased. "The most common and dangerous types of cognitive biases I have faced (and my description as most common and dangerous is a bias itself) are: Confirmation bias... Loss aversion bias... Outcome bias... Hindsight bias... Cascade bias... Instrumentation bias... Disposition bias... Dunning–Kruger effect... Courtesy bias... Ambiguity bias" ([Better for Less](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2017/08/better-for-less.html)).
8. **Direct, blunt naming of bad practice without euphemism.** Vendor and consultancy behavior gets called what it is. "Unfortunately, and there is no polite way of saying this, it's a lucrative scam" ([Doctrine](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2016/08/doctrine.html)); "This was dogma run wild" ([Better for Less](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2017/08/better-for-less.html)).

## Structure and formatting

Chapters and posts open in one of two ways: a dated, specific scene already in motion — "For me this journey begins two decades ago in the lift of the Arts hotel in Barcelona" ([On being lost](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/on-being-lost-2ef5f05eb1ec)) — or a flat statement of the practical problem to be solved, as in "The problem I had was how do I map a business?" ([Finding a path](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/finding-a-path-cdb1249078c0)). The book chapters are staged as a chronological narrative-of-discovery: each chapter picks up exactly where the last one's ignorance left off ("I had created my first map and applied an understanding of some basic climatic patterns..." opens [Doctrine](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2016/08/doctrine.html), continuing directly from Finding a Path), so the argument accumulates the way a memoir accumulates rather than the way a textbook stacks claims. Figures are not illustrations of the prose — they are load-bearing arguments the prose narrates around; nearly every section is built as "here is the map, now let me walk you through what it shows" (e.g., the ten labeled figures walking from user needs to a finished map in [Finding a path](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/finding-a-path-cdb1249078c0)), and named sub-headers like "The importance of maps in military history," "A game of chess," and "Categorising situational awareness" break the chapter into discovery-stage headings rather than topic labels. Numbered lists carry two different jobs depending on context: doctrine and climatic-pattern lists ("Doctrine: Focus on user need," "Doctrine: Be transparent," [Doctrine](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2016/08/doctrine.html)) are principle statements, bolded and titled like section headers in their own right, while step lists in tutorial posts are literally numbered actions the reader is told to go and do ("Step 1 — Needs," "Step 2 — Value Chain," [An introduction to Wardley (Value Chain) Mapping](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2015/02/an-introduction-to-wardley-value-chain.html)). Nearly every book chapter closes with a direct, second-person homework assignment headed "An exercise for the reader" that tells the reader to go map their own business before continuing — "Take a break, read this chapter again, pick a part of your business and have a go at mapping it... If within that time, you don't feel you're learning... then stop. You can recover your lost time by simply not reading any more chapters. Pick this book up, aim for the refuse bin and with a shout of 'that was a complete waste' then let it fly" ([Finding a path](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/finding-a-path-cdb1249078c0)) — a closing move that dares the reader to quit rather than begging them to stay. Standalone blog essays instead often end on a flat, undercutting caveat or a P.S. rather than a rallying call: "P.S. For everyone's sake, someone please come up with a better name than serverless" ([Why the fuss about serverless?](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2016/11/why-fuss-about-serverless.html)).

## Reference palette

Military history and doctrine dominate: Sun Tzu's *Art of War* and its five factors, John Boyd's OODA loop ("the mad major himself"), the American Civil War (Ball's Bluff, topographical intelligence), the Battle of Thermopylae and the "Hot Gates," Deng Xiaoping's "crossing the river by feeling the stones," and constant vocabulary borrowed from combat — flanking, suppressing fire, fool's mate, troops, terrain, inertia barriers, "points of war." Chess supplies a second, tightly braided register — the two "whys" of purpose versus movement, the board as the thing a bad strategist never sees, the "Chess World" thought experiment of playing without ever perceiving the board ([On being lost](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/on-being-lost-2ef5f05eb1ec)). Industrial and economic history is the third major vein: Maudslay's screw-cutting lathe (1800), the Portsmouth block-making machinery and the Armory Method, Thomas Thwaites's nine-month, thousand-pound attempt to build a toaster from raw ore, and the evolution of Nokia from paper mill to telecommunications giant — all used to argue that standardized components, not heroics, are what let civilizations build complex things. Cognitive and behavioral science supplies a named-bias vocabulary (confirmation bias, outcome bias, hindsight bias, Dunning–Kruger, false consensus bias) deployed with the same rigor as the military references, always to diagnose organizational self-deception rather than as decoration. Government and institutional bureaucracy (UK Cabinet Office, "Big IT," Whitehall departmentalism) supplies his most class-inflected, bluntest material. Notably underused compared to peers writing in the same space: pop-culture references, sports analogies unrelated to chess, and appeals to celebrity-CEO wisdom — when he does mention famous companies (Nokia, Kodak, Blockbuster, Amazon), it's always to extract a specific mechanism of inertia or evolution, never as an inspirational "be like them" gesture.

## Sentence-level habits

Long, cumulative, comma-and-em-dash-chained sentences that build up a full inventory of a mess before naming the mess — "We had five factors from purpose to landscape to climate to doctrine to leadership and somehow I had been jumping from purpose to leadership and missing three of them" ([On being lost](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/on-being-lost-2ef5f05eb1ec)) — are routinely punctuated by a short, flat sentence that lands the verdict: "It sucked." "The problem was me." "We built it." "I'd flee in panic." A recurring construction poses a rhetorical question and then answers it in the very next clause or sentence rather than leaving it to hang — "But how?" followed immediately by narrated action, or "Why toasters?" followed by the Thomas Thwaites story ([Finding a path](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/finding-a-path-cdb1249078c0)). Numbered "Point 1 / Point 2 / Point 3" callouts referencing a specific figure are a structural tic that appears in nearly every chapter, functioning as inline footnotes to the diagram rather than as a literary device. Colon-led lists using "-" or ":-" are frequent and slightly old-fashioned in punctuation style (British usage throughout — "organise," "industrialise," "recognise," "colour" implied by spelling conventions). Self-quotation of his own past bad reasoning is common, rendered in scare quotes to needle it: describing his own team's justifications as "core" or lacking "reasonable ROI," or noting a supplier's excuse "if you had specified it correctly in the first place." Dry, deadpan asides land mid-argument without changing register, e.g. calling his own theory "code for 'it might just be a fluke'" ([Doctrine](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2016/08/doctrine.html)) or noting of a failed bet on television technology, "Well, some you win, some you lose" ([The play and a decision to act](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/the-play-and-a-decision-to-act-8eb796b1dff1)). Parentheticals routinely carry a caveat or joke rather than pure clarification — "(a state school kid who had lived on a council estate)," "(this is large format)," "(and my description as most common and dangerous is a bias itself)."

## Signature moves

1. **The side-by-side diagram indictment.** Place two competing representations of the same situation next to each other and let the visual contrast do the arguing instead of stating a verdict outright. Example: the map of Thermopylae set beside a SWOT diagram of the same battle, followed by "I'd flee in panic" ([On being lost](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/on-being-lost-2ef5f05eb1ec)).
2. **The Sun Tzu five-factor retrofit.** Take an existing strategic framework (Sun Tzu's purpose/landscape/climate/doctrine/leadership) and use it as literal structural scaffolding for an entirely modern business argument, then point out exactly which factors business writing skips. "I could see a purpose and then a huge jump into leadership and the strategic choices we had made. But where was landscape, climate and doctrine?" ([On being lost](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/on-being-lost-2ef5f05eb1ec)).
3. **The named-and-defined taxonomy.** Whenever a concept has variants (biases, doctrine, gameplay), give each one a bolded name and a one-line definition rather than a vague gesture at "several factors." Example: the ten cognitive biases enumerated and defined in sequence in [Better for Less](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2017/08/better-for-less.html).
4. **The pioneers/settlers/town-planners cast of characters.** Personify a technical, structural idea (how organizations must handle constant evolution) as three named character types with distinct virtues, so the model can be taught, remembered, and argued about like people. "Pioneers are brilliant people. They are able to explore never before discovered concepts, the uncharted land... In the past, we often burnt them at the stake" ([On Pioneers, Settlers, Town Planners and Theft](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2015/03/on-pioneers-settlers-town-planners-and.html)).
5. **The dated confession that ends a chapter.** Close a chapter's narrative arc on a plain, undecorated admission of personal failure rather than a lesson dressed as a win. "The problem was me... Talk about a rookie mistake. I had finally been rumbled as that imposter CEO... My position was untenable and I resigned" ([The play and a decision to act](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/the-play-and-a-decision-to-act-8eb796b1dff1)).
6. **The homework dare.** End instructional chapters by directly challenging the reader to test the method themselves within hours, and explicitly invite them to throw the book away if it doesn't work. "Pick this book up, aim for the refuse bin and with a shout of 'that was a complete waste' then let it fly" ([Finding a path](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/finding-a-path-cdb1249078c0)).

## What NOT to do (what Wardley never does)

- He never states a framework's conclusion without first re-living the specific ignorance it corrected — the technique is never presented as free-standing wisdom, only as the fix for a dated personal failure.
- He never claims predictive certainty or guru status — even his most confident forecasting chapter opens with "I don't claim to have mystical powers of anticipation... I'm a lousy prognosticator" ([Anticipation](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2016/12/anticipation.html)).
- He never separates a diagram from the argument as mere decoration — nearly every claim is anchored to a specific numbered figure the prose is actively narrating, not illustrating after the fact.
- He never resume-flexes or lingers on credentials — being "voted one of the most influential people in IT within the UK" appears as a single clause in a sentence about his earlier confusion, not a boast ([An introduction to Wardley (Value Chain) Mapping](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2015/02/an-introduction-to-wardley-value-chain.html)).
- He never hides or reframes his own failures as secretly-successful pivots — the Zimki story ends in resignation and the company's dissolution, not a redemption arc grafted on afterward.
- He never uses corporate euphemism for bad practice — outsourcing scams, government waste, and vendor lock-in are named directly ("a lucrative scam," "dogma run wild") rather than softened into "areas for improvement."
- He never treats his own model as complete or final — doctrine, gameplay, and climatic-pattern lists are consistently flagged as partial ("this is not an exhaustive list," "there will always exist better doctrine in the future").
- He never reaches for pop-culture, celebrity, or sports references outside chess — the palette stays inside military history, industrial history, chess, cognitive science, and his own lived business record.

## How this differs from adjacent writers

Against Clayton Christensen, who treats disruption as a largely unforeseeable shock that ambushes incumbents, Wardley insists disruption splits into predictable and non-predictable forms and that the predictable kind — driven by components crossing from product to commodity — can be anticipated years in advance with named weak signals: "Town Planners often disrupt past industries. This can be anticipated quite a time in advance... There are two forms of disruption (predictable and non predictable) but that doesn't stop people pretending there is one" ([On Pioneers, Settlers, Town Planners and Theft](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2015/03/on-pioneers-settlers-town-planners-and.html)). Against Michael Porter or Richard Rumelt, whose strategy frameworks remain verbal and diagrammed only as supporting illustration, Wardley's map with its evolution axis is not an illustration of the argument but the argument itself — the prose exists to narrate a picture the reader must look at to follow the logic. Against Alexander Osterwalder's Business Model Canvas, which Wardley treats as a late, optional, largely dispensable add-on rather than a starting point: "I might use a SWOT or Business Model Canvas (BMC) to refine a few more details... I often omit this step" ([An introduction to Wardley (Value Chain) Mapping](https://blog.gardeviance.org/2015/02/an-introduction-to-wardley-value-chain.html)). Against the confident, aphoristic register of consultant-strategists generally, Wardley's authority is built entirely from a documented record of his own confusion and failure rather than from polished pronouncement — he would rather show the reader the SWOT diagram that made him "flee in panic" than assert a lesson from a position of unearned certainty.
